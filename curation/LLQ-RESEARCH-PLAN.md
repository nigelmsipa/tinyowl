> **Questions:**
> - **llq-001** — Obedience or Legalism? What's the Difference Between Them?
>   *Episode context: A viewer asks whether obeying God's commands — specifically regarding media — is legalism. The episode defines legalism as "putting law above the gospel" and distinguishes it from obedience motivated by love and saved status, using 1 John 3:4 (sin = lawlessness), the Eden narrative, and Deuteronomy 6:24. The key argument is relational: do you obey because you have to, or because you already are saved? Applied directly to media: keeping God's standards for what you view is not legalism, it is doing what God asks for your own benefit. Research must establish: the biblical law-grace distinction in SDA theology, the difference between works-righteousness and faith-driven obedience, and how Philippians 2:12-13 and Romans 8 frame willing obedience.*

> **Questions:**
> - **llq-002** — Impressions： God vs. Devil?
>   *Episode context: The actual question is: "How do I know my thoughts are my own, and how can I distinguish impressions from God vs. the devil?" The episode proposes three buckets — God's thoughts, the enemy's thoughts, and our own thoughts (which are influenced by one of the two) — arguing that thoughts don't truly originate with us. Key scriptures: Jeremiah 17:9 (deceitful heart), John 8:44 (devil is father of lies), Isaiah 55:8-9, Jeremiah 29:11, John 10:10. Tests offered: destructive thoughts = enemy; life-giving thoughts = God. Solution: recalibrate by immersing in Scripture. The media angle is implicit — what you consume shapes which category of thought dominates. Research must establish: the biblical theology of spiritual discernment, SOP passages on the origin of thoughts and media's role in shaping the mind, and how to practically apply Scripture as the arbiter of impressions.*
> - **llq-003** — Powerful Illustration About Hope - Have I Been Exposed to Too Much Darkness?
>   *Episode context: The actual question is: "Is there any hope for me? My mind is crowded with damaging music, films, and pornographic material — I can't even conceive of life without it." The episode uses a cup-of-water illustration (mind = cup, bad media = food coloring that clouds it, God = bleach that clears it). Key scriptures: Isaiah 41:10 ("do not be dismayed, I am your God"), Revelation 3:20 (Jesus knocking at the door), Deuteronomy 30:19 (choose life). God can create a new mind, heart, and spirit, but He waits for the door to be opened. Research must establish: the theology of mind renewal and sanctification, SOP on the power of habits and grace to overcome media-formed patterns, and the neuroscience of addiction recovery framed through a biblical lens.*

**Suggested sources:** *The Great Controversy*, *Desire of Ages*, *Mind, Character, and Personality*, SDA Bible Commentary

> Address each question as its own entry.

> 1. A direct declarative answer
> 2. The scripture(s) that anchor it
> 3. The theological argument
> 4. Historical context where relevant
> 5. Address the strongest counter-argument
>
> **Questions:**
> - **llq-004** — Not a Microchip - Mark of the Beast, What is It?
>   *Episode context: A viewer asks whether the mark is a device (microchip, 5G, mind control) and whether we can be tricked into taking it. The episode builds a detailed biblical argument: Revelation 13:16 and 14:9-10 link the mark to worship; Exodus 13:9 and Deuteronomy 6:5-8, 11:18 establish the forehead/hand motif as symbolic of thoughts (mind) and actions (hand); Ephesians 4:30 and Ezekiel 36:27 show the Spirit seals those whose minds and actions align with God's law. Conclusion: the mark is about conscious worship allegiance — it cannot be accidentally received. Research must establish: the full SDA theology of the mark of the beast and seal of God, the Sunday law connection, how the sanctuary typology informs the seal/mark contrast, and why physicality of the mark is a red herring.*
> - **llq-005** — What Is Predictive Programming?
>   *Episode context: The episode defines predictive programming (after Alan Watt) as subtle psychological conditioning via media to pre-familiarize the public with planned societal changes. LLS argues the real origin is demonic: Satan pre-imitated Christ through pagan figures (Horus, Dionysus, Krishna, Mithra — claimed virgin birth, death, resurrection) to desensitize people before the real fulfillment. Today's entertainment media continues this by promoting lower passions, portraying rule-keeping as archaic, and elevating feeling over rational-moral thought. Mitigation: distance from entertainment + prayer. Research must establish: the biblical theology of Satan's deceptive strategy in history, a careful scholarly evaluation of the pagan-Christ parallels claim (this is contested — research needs to be historically precise), and SOP on how imagination and storytelling shape spiritual receptivity.*
> - **llq-006** — Conspiracy or Truth?
>   *Episode context: A viewer challenges LLS: "A lot of what you share seems unbelievable — are you sure it's true?" The episode responds by invoking the two-or-three-witness principle and the Berean methodology (Acts 17:11 — search the scriptures to verify). LLS does not ask viewers to trust them on faith; they invite independent verification. Deuteronomy 29:29 is cited ("secret things belong to God; revealed things belong to us"). Their stated purpose is to point people to Scripture for evaluating the times. Research must establish: the biblical epistemology of discernment and evidence (Berean principle, multiple witnesses, testing prophets), how to evaluate fringe claims against Scripture and history, and the distinction between healthy critical thinking and conspiratorial excess.*

>
> **Questions:**
> - **llq-007** — Overcoming Addictions (even good ones)
>   *Episode context: The actual question is: "I think I'm addicted to Christian videos — I binge-watch them compulsively and feel cravings. Is this common? What should I do?" The episode offers four practical steps: (1) use God's promises in prayer during cravings; (2) change how you engage with content (audio while active, follow along with an open Bible); (3) create structured boundaries and reward systems; (4) find an accountability partner. The central warning: when you cut something out, fill the void with good things or something worse will fill it (Matthew 12:43-45 implied). Research must establish: the biblical theology of temperance and self-control (Galatians 5:22-23), SOP on the formation and reformation of habits, and the spiritual danger of even good things becoming an idol when consumed without discipline.*
> - **llq-008** — How Much Screen Time Is Safe for Kids?
>   *Episode context: The question is whether any screen time is safe for children. The episode cites the American Academy of Pediatrics (no screens under age 2) and the ABCD study (no screens under 18 months; no more than 2 hours/day for older children). Key qualifications: content should be educational; children should not consume screens alone but with an adult guide; video calls have different effects than entertainment programming. Bottom line: less is better; when consumed, do it together and choose educational content. Research must establish: the biblical theology of protecting the developing mind (Proverbs 22:6, Deuteronomy 6:6-7), SOP on education and the formative years, and the psychological/developmental case for why passive entertainment media is uniquely harmful to young children.*

## LLQ-G14 — Church Life, Worship, Media, and Ministry (7)

**Suggested sources:** *The Great Controversy*, *Desire of Ages*, *Mind, Character, and Personality*, SDA Bible Commentary

**Research prompt:**

> Address each question as its own entry.
>
> For EACH question, provide:
> 1. A direct declarative answer
> 2. The scripture(s) that anchor it
> 3. The theological argument
> 4. Historical context where relevant
> 5. Address the strongest counter-argument
>
> **Questions:**
> - **llq-009** — I'm Considering Making a TV Show?
>   *Episode context: A Christian artist/actor wants to make a fantasy TV series shaped around the Bible and the Great Controversy. Scottie Meyer (10 years in Hollywood, film school graduate) answers from personal experience. Key principles: (1) all art is meant to influence — the question is what you want people to walk away with; (2) content doesn't need to say "Jesus" explicitly but must align with biblical values; (3) allegory is valid, but the episode cautions against including elements the Bible condemns (e.g., magic and sorcery) unless they are portrayed with clear moral consequence; (4) collaborators matter — wrong creative partners can steer the project off-course. Anchored in Matthew 5:13 (salt of the earth) and 1 Corinthians 10:31. Research must establish: the theology of Christian culture-making and artistic vocation, SOP on imagination and the limits of fiction, and the biblical theology of allegory (from Ezekiel to Revelation) as a legitimate vehicle for truth.*
> - **llq-010** — Why Do Ya'll Hate Hollywood So Much?
>   *Episode context: The episode is a personal testimony — Scottie Meyer spent 10 years in Hollywood and loved it before God changed his tastes. He distinguishes between hating Hollywood's people (he doesn't) and its content (he does). The argument: Hollywood's content is primarily contrary to biblical values; God can change your tastes if you give him permission; the appropriate response is prayer for those in the industry. "As you draw closer to God, the things of this world will grow strangely dim in the light of his glory and grace." Research must establish: the theology of Christian engagement with culture (transformationist, separatist, and redemptive models), SOP on the moral influence of entertainment, and the biblical basis for distinguishing entertainment that corrupts from that which ennobles.*
> - **llq-011** — Can I Use My Media Skills for God?
>   *Episode context: A self-taught media professional asks how to transition from using skills for the world to using them for God. Michael (LLS team member, media skills honed during COVID) shares personal testimony of offering his skills to his church when job-seeking failed, which eventually led to working at LLS. Key argument: using skills for God and for the world don't conflict — as long as the work is biblically grounded. You don't need to be in a church or ministry; start small (photograph creation, write encouraging posts, record moments of love). Research must establish: the theology of vocation and the sanctification of ordinary work (Colossians 3:23), SOP on the stewardship of talents and the priesthood of all believers, and the biblical principle that all of life — including creative work — can be an act of worship.*
> - **llq-012** — How Do I Identify Unsuitable Media?
>   *Episode context: The episode uses Philippians 4:8 as the primary filter — true, noble, just, pure, lovely, of good report, virtuous, praiseworthy — and applies each criterion step-by-step to media choices. Marvel's The Eternals is used as a specific negative example (identified as gnostic). Psalm 101:3 is also cited ("I will set nothing wicked before my eyes"). The bottom-line practical test: "Will it draw me closer to God or away?" Research must establish: the exegesis of Philippians 4:8 in its context, the history of gnostic themes in mainstream entertainment, SOP on the standard for what the mind should dwell upon, and the theological definition of "wicked" as that which is against God's law.*
> - **llq-013** — How Do I Talk to My Friends About Harmful Media?
>   *Episode context: The actual question is: "How do I open a conversation with a friend about the dangers of certain media?" The episode counsels a gentle, non-confrontational approach: begin with your own personal limits ("it does something to my spirit"); find common ground before raising biblical concerns; if the person is a Christian, then bring in Matthew 5:27-29 (sin starts in the mind with lust; Jesus says it's better to pluck out your eye). Two practical barometers: does the content glamorize sin? Does it break the Ten Commandments? Research must establish: the biblical theology of gentle correction (Galatians 6:1, 2 Timothy 2:24-26), SOP on the social power of influence and the responsibility of those with knowledge, and the psychology of desensitization — how repeated visual exposure to sin normalizes it.*
> - **llq-014** — I Stopped Watching Mainstream Media, Now What?
>   *Episode context: Brittany (LLS team) shares a personal testimony of social media addiction in her youth — it consumed her life, affected her sleep, homework, and relationship with God. She tried to quit cold turkey and failed within two hours. The key insight: stopping a toxic habit is only half the battle; the failure comes from focusing on what you are leaving behind rather than what lies ahead. Isaiah 43:19 ("I am doing a new thing") is the anchor. Practical advice: (1) ask God to show you how to fill the time and make a list of forward-looking activities; (2) be patient — it took her five years. Research must establish: the theology of renewal and putting on the new self (Ephesians 4:22-24), SOP on replacing harmful habits with godly ones, and the principle that nature abhors a spiritual vacuum — substitution, not just cessation.*
> - **llq-015** — My House is Divided - How can we be unified on not watching harmful media?
>   *Episode context: Scottie Meyer shares how early in his marriage, his wife (who grew up with TV as constant background noise) and he (quickly convicted about media content) were in conflict. His strategy: rather than confronting directly, he suggested alternative activities (walks, board games), removed her from the media-watching context, then gradually shared information as questions ("I came across this study — what do you think?"). He found common ground by asking what makes content good before raising the question of whether bad content exists. Over time, God changed her heart — her standards are now stricter than his. Research must establish: the biblical theology of leading a household with patient persuasion (1 Peter 3:1-2, Ephesians 5:25-27), SOP on the home as a sanctuary and the husband/parent's spiritual responsibility, and why coercive rule-enforcement in the home backfires while relational trust succeeds.*

> - **llq-016** — Am I Being Too Strict With My Children?
>   *Episode context: The actual questions are: "Will not allowing my children to watch mainstream TV cause them to rebel? Should I let them watch it in front of me so they're not hiding it?" The episode affirms that parental guidance and restrictions are fully biblical (Proverbs 4:10-13 — parents are to teach, guide, and guard). But will they rebel? Honestly — yes, possibly. The episode argues that parameters alone don't prevent rebellion; the decisive factor is the relational foundation of trust and honesty.
