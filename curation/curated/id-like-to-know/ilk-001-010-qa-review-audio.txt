Question: How does the 2300-day prophecy lead to 1844 if some argue that Scripture never teaches a day-for-a-year principle?

Answer:
The 2300-day prophecy reaches 1844 by applying the day-for-a-year principle to symbolic prophecy. Ezekiel 4:6 and Numbers 14:34 establish the biblical principle of a symbolic day standing for a year. Daniel 7:25 then shows symbolic prophetic time inside a chapter filled with symbols, so its time expression is understood symbolically as well.

The same principle also applies to the seventy weeks of Daniel 9, which are understood as years. From there, Daniel 9:25 supplies the starting point in 457 BC, the command to restore and build Jerusalem. On that basis, the 2300 days of Daniel 8:14 are understood as prophetic years extending to the autumn of 1844.

The eighth of Revelation 17:11 is not an eighth head separate from the seven. The beast has seven heads, not eight. The eighth is the resurrected persecuting phase of the seventh head.

The next step is to identify what the heads represent. Kings and kingdoms are treated as interchangeable in prophetic language, as in Daniel 2 and Daniel 7. Mountains also point to kingdoms. So the seven heads are not seven individual popes, but seven successive powers.

So the eighth is the papacy revived after the deadly wound, functioning again as a persecuting power through authority restored with the help of the United States.

Question: Does Psalm 104 teach that human beings should work only during the day and never at night?

Psalm 104 teaches a general pattern, not an inflexible universal ban on night work.

The psalm describes the normal rhythm of creation: certain animals move at night, and man goes forth to his labor by day. That does show that daylight is the ordinary time for human labor and nighttime is ordinarily fitted for rest.

So the answer is that day labor is the normal pattern, but Psalm 104 is not a law forbidding all night work in every circumstance.

Question: If you sell a house and use the proceeds to buy another house, should you tithe any of that money?

Answer:
You tithe the increase, not the entire sale price.

The reasoning is straightforward. If the money originally invested in the house was already tithed when it first came to you as income, that principal is not being tithed again. But if the property increased in value, that gain is increase, and the tithe belongs on that increase.

So if you bought the house for one amount and sold it for more, the extra value is treated here as the part on which tithe should be returned. The source also stresses that tithe is not generosity but honesty. Offerings are where generosity enters, because offerings are given freely beyond what already belongs to God.

So the answer is yes: if the sale produced real increase, tithe should be returned on that increase, even if the rest of the money is being rolled into another house purchase.

Question: What does the uncovering of feet mean in Ruth 3?

So the uncovering of feet means Ruth was making a symbolic and lawful appeal for Boaz to marry and redeem her. It does not justify treating the scene as premarital sexual license, and the source explicitly rejects using the passage that way.
