## Entry `ilk-004a`

- `question`: `Did the Jewish theocracy end in the days of Zedekiah, or did it continue until the close of the seventy-week prophecy in AD 34?`
- `scripture_basis`: `Daniel 9:24-27; Ezekiel 8-11; Haggai 2:9; Matthew 23:34-39; Acts 1:8; Acts 7-8; Acts 13:46-47`
- `tags`: `theocracy, Daniel-9, AD-34, Zedekiah, Shekinah, probation, Acts, Jewish-nation`

### Answer

The theocracy did not officially end in the days of Zedekiah. It ended in `AD 34`, after the additional probationary period tied to the seventy-week prophecy had run its course.

Before the Babylonian captivity, the Shekinah was in the temple. Ezekiel 8-11 then shows the abominations in Jerusalem and the departure of the divine glory from the city. That explains why some people place the end of the theocracy back there. But that cannot be the final end, because Haggai says the glory of the second temple would be greater than the first.

That greater glory is fulfilled when Christ Himself comes to the temple. In that sense, the Shekinah returns in the person of Jesus. At the close of His ministry, the pattern of Ezekiel is repeated: He leaves the temple, declares it desolate, and lingers on the Mount of Olives. Even then, however, mercy is not yet exhausted.

Matthew 23 shows that additional mercy still remains. Christ says He will send prophets, wise men, and scribes, and that this guilt will come upon that generation. The Jewish theocracy therefore receives another three and a half years of mercy after the cross.

Acts then supplies the historical structure. Acts 1 announces the movement of the gospel from Jerusalem and Judea to Samaria and then to the ends of the earth. Acts 7 records Stephen's stoning. Acts 8 turns outward, and Acts 13 explicitly says, `Lo, we turn to the Gentiles.` So the official close is placed in `AD 34`, not in the reign of Zedekiah.

## Entry `ilk-004b`

- `question`: `Who were the saints raised in the special resurrection connected with Christ's resurrection, and why is there so little detail about them?`
- `scripture_basis`: `Matthew 27:51-53; John 21:24-25; Ephesians 4:8; Psalm 68:18-25; Matthew 23:34-35`
- `secondary_support`: `The Desire of Ages, p. 786; Early Writings, p. 184`
- `tags`: `special-resurrection, resurrection, Matthew-27, Ephesians-4, Psalm-68, martyrs, ascension`

### Answer

The special resurrection was not a minor event. It was likely widely known, even if Scripture does not preserve every detail about it. John 21 reminds you that many things Jesus did were never written down in full. So the absence of extended narrative does not mean the event was obscure or insignificant.

Matthew 27:51-53 gives the event itself: graves were opened, many saints arose after Christ's resurrection, entered the holy city, and appeared to many. That much is explicit. From there, two lines of evidence are developed.

First, Ephesians 4:8 is connected with Psalm 68. When Christ ascended on high, He `led captivity captive.` That is taken as a biblical echo of the group raised with Him. Psalm 68 then broadens the picture into a triumphal procession moving into the sanctuary in connection with Christ's ascension.

Second, secondary support fills in details that Matthew does not spell out. *The Desire of Ages* says Christ brought `a multitude of captives` from the grave. *Early Writings* says they were glorified saints, chosen and holy ones from every age, from creation down to the days of Christ. They are therefore understood here as martyrs who had borne witness to the truth.

That is why Abel is treated as a likely member of that company, and John the Baptist is treated as the clearest martyr from the days of Christ who could belong to it. Matthew 23 strengthens that line of thought by naming righteous blood from Abel onward, while the secondary support extends the range to the days of Christ.

This was a glorified resurrection, not a mere return to ordinary mortal life. The group is understood as a multitude of martyr-witnesses raised with Christ, brought forth as trophies of His victory, and carried with Him in connection with His ascension.
