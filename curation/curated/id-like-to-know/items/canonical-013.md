## Entry `canonical-013a`

- `question`: `Is it wrong to show physical affection — stroking, holding — during church service, and how should people conduct themselves in the house of God?`
- `scripture_basis`: `1 Corinthians 14:40`
- `source_scripture_usage`: `1 Corinthians 14:40 is quoted directly: "Let all things be done decently and in order." No other passage is opened. The argument runs on the purpose of worship and the distraction principle rather than additional texts.`
- `secondary_support`: ``
- `tags`: `church-conduct, reverence, worship, affection, decency, order, 1-Corinthians-14, sanctuary, distraction`

### Answer

The purpose of the worship service is to focus on God — to pray, sing, hear the Word, and direct attention upward. Anything that pulls that focus away, whether from the person doing it or those sitting nearby, is out of place. 1 Corinthians 14:40 is the standard: "Let all things be done decently and in order."

Physical affection between spouses is not wrong. The place for it is not the sanctuary. Carrying private expressions of intimacy into the house of God dishonors the Lord and distracts the congregation. The same applies to texting, feeding children snacks during service, or anything else that makes the common crowd out the holy. Reverence takes different forms across cultures — some churches say amen aloud, others sit in silence — but mishandling your spouse in the pew is out of bounds everywhere. The house of God is a house of worship.

## Entry `canonical-013b`

- `question`: `In Revelation 5:6, Jesus is described as having seven eyes which are the seven spirits of God. Does this mean Jesus sees what is happening on earth through the Holy Spirit? And is the Holy Spirit a person or a force or energy coming from the Father and the Son?`

- `secondary_support`: `Ellen G. White, Bible Training School, March 1, 1906; Acts of the Apostles, pages 51-52; Ministry of Healing, page 417; Desire of Ages, page 352`
- `tags`: `Holy-Spirit, personhood, Revelation-5, seven-eyes, seven-spirits, omniscience, intercession, heavenly-sanctuary, Godhead, Trinity, John-16, John-3, angels, Ellen-White, Hebrews`

### Answer

On personhood there is no ambiguity. Ellen White names three living persons of the heavenly trio — Father, Son, and Holy Spirit — in Bible Training School, March 1, 1906. The Holy Spirit has intelligence, can be grieved, and is volitional; John 3:8 says the Spirit blows where he wills. He convicts, sanctifies, gives gifts. He is a person.

On the nature of the Holy Spirit, restraint is right. Ellen White writes in Acts of the Apostles pages 51-52 that "the nature of the Holy Spirit is a mystery. Men cannot explain it because the Lord has not revealed it to them." Silence is golden on the how.

On the seven eyes in Revelation 5:6: the image is figurative. Seven is the number of completeness or fullness throughout Revelation, and the seven eyes symbolizing the seven spirits of God express that Christ sees and knows all things fully — they are not a claim that he perceives the earth only through the Holy Spirit as a separate sensory organ he lacks. John 16:7 puts it from Christ's own mouth: "If I do not go away, the Helper will not come to you; but if I depart, I will send him to you." The Spirit's presence on earth is Christ's sending, not a sign of Christ's limitation.

As for how the Father and Son remain aware of all things while the Spirit ministers on earth: Ellen White in Ministry of Healing page 417 writes that God is not inert on his throne but surrounded by tens of thousands of holy beings waiting to do his will; "through these messengers he is in active communication with every part of his dominion. By his spirit, he is everywhere present." The angels extend divine communication throughout creation; the Spirit is everywhere. Desire of Ages page 352 similarly places all the intelligences of heaven in Christ's army. The scenario the questioner fears — that Jesus is effectively blind to the earth while interceding — does not hold. He is the interceding high priest in the heavenly sanctuary, the Spirit is his sent representative on earth, and the angelic host carries awareness throughout creation. These are distinct roles operating in concert, not a division that leaves any part of the creation unseen.
