## Entry `ilk-012a`

- `question`: `Did the soul of Jesus go up to the Father when He died?`
- `scripture_basis`: `John 10:17-18; John 20:17; 1 Corinthians 15:20`
- `secondary_support`: `Early Writings, pp. 187-188`
- `tags`: `Jesus, soul, state-of-the-dead, resurrection, firstfruits, ascension`

### Answer

No. The soul does not go anywhere at death, because the soul is not a separate conscious entity that survives when body and breath are parted.

Jesus said in John 10 that He would lay down His life and take it again, but in John 20 He told Mary, `I am not yet ascended to My Father.` That rules out the idea that His soul had already gone to heaven at death.

What did happen is that on resurrection morning Jesus quickly ascended to His Father to hear that His sacrifice was accepted and to receive authority in heaven and earth. That is linked with the feast of firstfruits and with 1 Corinthians 15, where Christ is called the firstfruits.

He did not die and send a conscious soul upward. He truly died, was raised by the Father, and then ascended in harmony with the firstfruits pattern.

## Entry `ilk-012b`

- `question`: `Was Jesus peaceable or impeccable?`
- `scripture_basis`: `Isaiah 9:6; Hebrews 4:14-16`
- `tags`: `Jesus, peaceable, impeccable, temptation, sinlessness, humanity`

### Answer

Jesus was peaceable, and He was also sinless.

He is peaceable because He is the Prince of Peace and gives His peace to His people. He is also spotless in the sense that Hebrews 4 says He was tempted in all points as we are, yet without sin.

But do not take that to mean that temptation was unreal or that there was no real moral conflict. He could have sinned, yet never did. He kept a perfect relationship with His Father and never allowed temptation to become sinful thought or sinful act.

So if `impeccable` simply means that He never sinned, yes. If it means that temptation was unreal and that sin was impossible to Him in any meaningful sense, that is not how His experience is being described here.

## Entry `ilk-012c`

- `question`: `Are there prophets for today, and do Joel 2 and Acts 2 teach that all true believers are prophets in the end time?`
- `scripture_basis`: `Joel 2:28-32; Acts 2:17-21; Ephesians 4:11-13`
- `secondary_support`: `Ellen White application of Joel 2 to the end time`
- `tags`: `prophets, prophecy, Joel-2, Acts-2, spiritual-gifts, end-times, Ellen-White`

### Answer

Ephesians 4 leaves room for prophets in the church until Christ comes. Joel 2 and Acts 2 also speak of sons and daughters prophesying, old men dreaming dreams, and young men seeing visions in the last days. So there can still be Spirit-given prophetic manifestations in needed times and circumstances.

But do not confuse that with the idea that every believer is a prophet in the technical sense, or that God must raise another lifelong prophetic figure exactly like Daniel, John, or Ellen White.

So do not deny that God may raise individuals to speak prophetically in particular circumstances. But do not flatten Joel 2 and Acts 2 into a blanket endorsement of every charismatic claim.

## Entry `ilk-012d`

- `question`: `How should you test people who claim to be prophets today?`
- `scripture_basis`: `Matthew 7:20; Isaiah 8:20; Deuteronomy 18:21-22; 1 John 4:1-3`
- `tags`: `false-prophets, testing-prophets, fruits, law-and-testimony, prophecy, discernment`

### Answer

Matthew 7 says, `By their fruits ye shall know them.` Isaiah 8 says, `To the law and to the testimony.` Deuteronomy says that what a prophet speaks in the name of the Lord must come to pass. First John says the true confession acknowledges Jesus Christ come in the flesh. So a genuine prophet must pass the whole biblical test, not just part of it.

That is why public prophetic personalities who contradict Scripture, fail the law-and-testimony test, chase money and scandal, or speak false predictions should not be received as true prophets.

So do not be gullible, and do not be cynical either. Test the claimant by Scripture. If even one of the divine tests fails, do not receive that person as a true prophet.
