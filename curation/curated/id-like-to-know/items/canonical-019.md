## Entry `canonical-019a`

- `question`: `Are church manuals and books of order replacing the Bible? Are we in danger of putting more authority in them than the Bible, hindering the Protestant principle of Scripture alone by teaching the commandments of men (Matthew 15:3-9)?`
- `scripture_basis`: `Matthew 15:3-9`
- `source_scripture_usage`: `Matthew 15:3-9 is referenced by the questioner regarding the commandments of men. The panel does not exposit specific verses in their response, relying instead on the Protestant principle of Sola Scriptura and practical ecclesiology.`
- `secondary_support`: `The Standish brothers (referenced in the question); Robert's Rules of Order (used as a comparison for the church's working policy).`
- `tags`: `church-manual, Sola-Scriptura, authority, church-organization, working-policy, Matthew-15`

### Answer

The Seventh-day Adventist Church maintains the firm Protestant position of the Bible and the Bible alone as its supreme authority. The Church Manual and other policy books do not replace, contradict, or supersede Scripture. They are only legitimate insofar as they harmonize with the Word of God.

The purpose of the Church Manual is not to establish new doctrines, but to serve as an operational guide to maintain order, protect sound doctrine, prevent the abuse of authority, and promote global unity. It provides the practical "nuts and bolts" for how the church operates—defining the roles of pastors, elders, and deacons, outlining how to conduct elections and ceremonies, and explaining how to handle church discipline biblically. 

There is a clear distinction between the Bible and these manuals. The Church Manual can only be amended by a vote of the full global delegation at a General Conference Session, ensuring broad consensus rather than arbitrary top-down decrees. There are also working policies for day-to-day administration (like salaries and properties) and a Minister's Manual for conducting ceremonies. Problems only arise when these manuals are applied unevenly—such as when local conferences ignore global policies or invent rules to ban speakers improperly. When applied correctly and kept subordinate to the Bible, the manuals are a necessary blessing that keeps a global movement united and functioning smoothly.

- `scripture_basis`: `Acts 5:29`

### Answer

This is a serious and difficult situation. The governing biblical principle when human authority contradicts God’s commands is found in Acts 5:29: "We ought to obey God rather than men." 

One approach is to persistently pray and lovingly but firmly talk to your husband, explaining that withholding the tithe robs God and blocks the financial and spiritual blessings He wants to pour out on your household. Sometimes, a non-believing spouse will eventually relent, allow the tithe to be paid, and subsequently witness God's blessing—which can even lead to their conversion. 

However, even if a husband never sees the light, the biblical requirement to be faithful remains. We are approaching a time in earth's history when believers will have to make incredibly tough decisions—choosing whether to serve God or face severe consequences, even the loss of life. If we compromise our faithfulness now under pressure, we are not preparing our characters for the ultimate crunch time ahead.
