## Entry `ilk-013a`

- `question`: `How would you answer claims that tithing does not apply to Christians today?`
- `scripture_basis`: `Matthew 23:23; 1 Corinthians 9:8-14; 1 Timothy 5:17-18; Hebrews 7:8; Genesis 14:20`
- `tags`: `tithe, stewardship, Matthew-23, 1-Corinthians-9, Hebrews-7, Melchizedek, church-support`

### Answer

Do not treat tithing as something that disappeared merely because the New Testament does not restate it as a brand-new command. Some practices were already taken for granted unless Scripture clearly set them aside.

Matthew 23 is the first text pressed into service. Jesus rebukes the Pharisees for majoring in minors, but He still says, `these ought ye to have done, and not to leave the other undone.` So the problem is not tithing itself. The problem is legalism that tithes while neglecting justice, mercy, and faith.

Paul then carries the principle forward in 1 Corinthians 9 and again in 1 Timothy 5. Those who minister holy things live from the holy things, and those who preach the gospel should live from the gospel. The ox is not to be muzzled while it treads out the grain. So support for gospel labor is not an expired Jewish relic.

Hebrews 7 strengthens the point by reaching back before Israel's ceremonial system. Abraham gave tithe to Melchizedek, and Hebrews says, `here men that die receive tithes; but there he receiveth them.` That means the principle predates the Levitical order and still stands in relation to Christ.

So tithing remains binding, not as a legalistic bargain for blessing, but as honesty toward God and as support for the gospel work.

## Entry `ilk-013b`

- `question`: `Are family games, beach outings, parties, or sports appropriate ways to keep the Sabbath?`
- `scripture_basis`: `Mark 2:27; Isaiah 58:13; Hebrews 10:25`
- `tags`: `Sabbath, recreation, family, games, beach, worship, Isaiah-58`

### Answer

The Sabbath was made for man, but that does not mean you are free to fill it with your own pleasure. Isaiah 58 governs the reasoning here: honor the Sabbath by turning away from doing your own ways, finding your own pleasure, and speaking your own words.

That does not mean the day is supposed to be cold or joyless. Spend time with family, worship together, study the Bible, and if possible go into nature and let creation teach the things of God. In that sense, family time belongs on the Sabbath when it deepens communion with God rather than replacing it.

So keep a sharp line between spiritually directed refreshment and entertainment. Walking in a quiet natural setting and teaching spiritual lessons is different from swimming, ball games, goofy competitions, parties, or other activities that simply recreate the atmosphere of an ordinary pleasure day.

Assembly is also part of the reasoning. Do not neglect gathering with the church and then justify skipping worship in the name of family balance. The family is not helped by replacing God's holy time with secular amusement.

So the limit is not whether people are smiling or together. The limit is whether the activity turns the Sabbath toward God or away from Him. If it serves communion, worship, and holy delight, it fits the day. If it is basically your own pleasure dressed up as family fun, it does not.
