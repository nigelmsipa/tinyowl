## Entry `canonical-007a`

- `question`: `Why did the veil separating the holy and most holy place in the heavenly sanctuary continue to exist until 1844? How do we reconcile the rending of the earthly veil at the cross with Christ's two-phase priestly ministry in heaven?`

### Answer

The rending of the veil at Christ's death signified the end of the earthly sanctuary system, not the abolition of the heavenly sanctuary or its phases. Hebrews 8 says the earthly tabernacle was only a type and shadow of the true heavenly reality. When Jesus died, the veil of His flesh was torn, putting an end to the validity of earthly animal sacrifices in the sense explained by Daniel 9 and opening direct access to God through Christ.

However, the heavenly sanctuary still operates with two distinct apartments. The book of Revelation shows Jesus first ministering in the holy place, walking among the candlesticks and standing at the altar of incense. Later, in 1844, Revelation 3 describes a door being closed (the holy place) and an open door being placed before the church (the most holy place), transitioning Christ's ministry to the final phase of judgment where the Ark of His Testament is seen (Revelation 11:19). The torn veil on earth simply meant the shadow had met its reality in the death of the Son of God; it did not collapse the sequential ministry Christ was about to begin in heaven.

## Entry `canonical-007b`

- `question`: `Who served as the high priest in the heavenly sanctuary before Jesus's inauguration as our high priest at His ascension?`
- `scripture_basis`: `Daniel 9:24; Hebrews 5:1-6; Romans 3:21-26`
- `source_scripture_usage`: `Daniel 9:24 is read to show the anointing of the most holy place. Hebrews 5:1-6 is read to establish the qualifications for high priesthood, emphasizing that Christ had to experience human weakness and offer a sacrifice before being inaugurated. Romans 3:21-26 is read to explain that God passed over Old Testament sins in forbearance until the actual propitiation of Christ's blood.`
- `secondary_support`: `Ellen G. White, Early Writings 126 (Jesus communing with the Father after the fall to obtain permission to be the ransom)`
- `tags`: `high-priest, Old-Testament-salvation, Hebrews-5, Romans-3, intercessor, inauguration, Daniel-9`

### Answer

Strictly speaking, no one served as a fully inaugurated high priest before Christ, because the blood of bulls and goats could not actually remove sin. In the Old Testament, God passed over sins on credit, as Romans 3:25 explains. Salvation was always through faith in the coming Savior, whose future sacrifice guaranteed their pardon.

Jesus was the designated intercessor in God's plan even before sin existed, and He began interceding for humanity the moment Adam and Eve fell. However, to officially become the High Priest, He had to meet specific qualifications: He had to become human, experience our weaknesses, and offer a real, effective sacrifice for sin, which is the point made in Hebrews 5. Only after dying on the cross could He ascend to heaven with His own blood to formally anoint the heavenly sanctuary, in line with Daniel 9:24, and be inaugurated as our High Priest, turning the promise of Old Testament salvation into a paid reality.
