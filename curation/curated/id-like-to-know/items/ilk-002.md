## Entry `ilk-002a`

- `question`: `Where was Satan when God created Adam and Eve? Did Satan's fall happen before creation or after?`
- `scripture_basis`: `Revelation 12:7`
- `secondary_support`: `Story of Redemption, pp. 18-19; Early Writings, p. 145`
- `tags`: `Satan, creation, fall, heaven, chronology, great-controversy`

### Answer

Revelation 12:7 shows that there was a war in heaven and that Satan was cast out, but the verse itself does not specify the exact timing in relation to creation. Secondary support then fills in the sequence: war in heaven first, expulsion from heaven next, and then the creation of the earth and humanity.

On that reading, Satan and his angels were cast out before the creation of Adam and Eve. Satan was not cast directly onto the earth immediately in order to rule it. Rather, after humanity fell, he claimed the world in a new way through Adam's failure.

## Entry `ilk-002b`

- `question`: `Should the number 666 be attributed to the United States of America or to the papacy?`
- `scripture_basis`: `Revelation 13:16-18`
- `tags`: `666, beast, image-of-the-beast, papacy, united-states, Sunday-law, Revelation-13`

### Answer

Revelation 13:18 links the number 666 to the beast, so the number is attributed to the papal power rather than to the United States. Revelation 13 connects the image of the beast back to the first beast, and the first beast is identified in this interpretation as the papacy.

The United States functions as the power that helps form or enforce the image of the beast, but not as the primary referent of the number 666 itself. In this line of interpretation, the mark of the beast and Sunday sacredness are tied back to papal authority, so 666 belongs directly to the papacy.

## Entry `ilk-002c`

- `question`: `Can tares become wheat, and can wheat become tares, in the parable of the wheat and the tares?`
- `scripture_basis`: `Matthew 13:24-30; Matthew 13:36-43`
- `tags`: `parable, wheat-and-tares, conversion, apostasy, harvest, interpretation`

### Answer

The parable of the wheat and the tares does not give a clear enough statement to answer that question dogmatically, so a definite answer would go beyond the text. The issue remains hypothetical because the parable's main emphasis is the final separation at harvest, not the mechanics of personal transition from one category to another.

At the same time, do not assume that someone who appears to be a tare now will necessarily remain that way, because the final distinction is fully revealed only at the harvest. Lived experience leaves room for the possibility of conversion, even though the parable itself does not settle the question with an explicit statement.

## Entry `ilk-002d`

- `question`: `What evidence is there that Satan was cast down twice in relation to Revelation 12: first from heaven originally, and later at the cross as earth's representative?`
- `scripture_basis`: `Revelation 12:10-11; John 12:30-33; Luke 10:18`
- `secondary_support`: `SDA Bible Commentary, vol. 5, p. 1150`
- `tags`: `Revelation-12, Satan, cross, John-12, Luke-10, great-controversy, heavenly-sanctuary`

### Answer

Revelation 12:10-11 provides the first evidence for a second casting down, because it speaks about Satan accusing the brethren and about believers overcoming him by the blood of the Lamb. That language points to the time of Christ's sacrifice, not merely to Satan's original expulsion from heaven.

John 12:30-33 supports the same reading, because Jesus says, in connection with His being lifted up, that the ruler of this world will be cast out. Luke 10:18 stands alongside that theme, and secondary support then reinforces it by stating that when Christ cried, "It is finished," Satan fell like lightning from heaven.

The first casting out is the original expulsion from heaven, while the second is Satan's loss of standing and claim at the cross. After the cross, Christ stands as the rightful representative of this world in place of Adam's lost position.
