## Entry `ilk-017a`

- `question`: `Are Christians taking Old Testament messianic prophecies out of context when they apply texts like Hosea 11, Psalm 22, and Isaiah 53 to Christ?`
- `scripture_basis`: `Hosea 11:1; Matthew 2:14-15; Psalm 22; Isaiah 53; 2 Corinthians 3:13-16; Daniel 9`
- `tags`: `messianic-prophecy, typology, Hosea-11, Psalm-22, Isaiah-53, Judaism, Christ, Daniel-9`

### Answer

No. Those passages are not being ripped out of context when they are applied to Christ. They are being read in the way Scripture itself reads them.

Hosea 11 works typologically. Israel was God's son called out of Egypt, and Matthew shows that this pattern reached its fuller fulfillment in Christ. The type is not identical to the antitype, but the earlier event points forward to the greater one.

Psalm 22 and Isaiah 53 press the point even harder. Psalm 22 is called a psalm of David, yet the details outrun David's own life. There is no event in David's experience where his hands and feet were pierced, his garments were divided, and the mockers at his death spoke the words echoed at the cross. The psalm points beyond David to the suffering Messiah.

The same is true of Isaiah 53. Israel did not bear the iniquity of us all. Israel was not wounded for our transgressions and offered as a guilt offering for the sins of others. The servant in Isaiah 53 suffers in a substitutionary way that fits Christ, not the nation as a whole.

Second Corinthians 3 explains why this is often resisted. A veil remains when the Old Testament is read without Christ, but that veil is taken away in Him. So keep opening the Scriptures, pray for that veil to be removed, and let the Old Testament speak honestly. If you do, Christ is not being forced into those passages. He is already there.

## Entry `ilk-017b`

- `question`: `What marks the beginning of marriage: the vows or the consummation?`
- `scripture_basis`: `Genesis 2; Genesis 4:1; Exodus 19:5-8; Matthew 19:6`
- `tags`: `marriage, vows, consummation, covenant, Genesis, Exodus-19`

### Answer

Marriage begins with the covenant vows, not with consummation.

Genesis gives that order. God joined Adam and Eve as husband and wife, and then later Genesis 4:1 says that Adam knew Eve and she conceived. The covenant came first; sexual union followed as the privilege belonging to that covenant.

Exodus 19 helps illustrate the same structure. God proposed covenant terms to Israel, and when the people said, `All that the Lord has spoken we will do,` the covenant was established. In the same way, marriage begins when a man and a woman publicly bind themselves to one another in covenant.

That is why consummation is not what creates the marriage. It belongs inside the marriage. If two people make the covenant before God and witnesses, they are married even if consummation is delayed.

So do not treat the vows as secondary and sex as the real maker of the marriage. The vows are the decisive act. What God has joined together, let not man separate.
