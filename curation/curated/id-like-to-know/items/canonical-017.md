## Entry `canonical-017a`

- `question`: `When a sinner comes to repentance and turns their life around, is that person still labeled a sinner?`
- `scripture_basis`: `Romans 5:1; Romans 8:1; Romans 4:3; Galatians 3:5-6; 1 John 2:1; 1 John 3:6, 9`

- `secondary_support`: ``
- `tags`: `justification, sanctification, sinner, righteousness, imputed-righteousness, Romans-5, Romans-8, Romans-4, Galatians-3, 1-John-2, 1-John-3, assurance, repentance, simul-iustus-et-peccator, Luther, advocate`

### Answer

Technically yes and no — and that tension is the heart of justification by faith. Romans 5:1 is the ground: "Having been justified by faith, we have peace with God through our Lord Jesus Christ." Justification means God looks at the repentant, believing sinner and declares them righteous — not because sin has ceased, but because Christ's righteousness is credited to their account. Romans 8:1 says it from the other direction: "There is therefore now no condemnation to those who are in Christ Jesus."

Luther captured the paradox in Latin: *simul iustus et peccator* — at the same time righteous, at the same time a sinner. When is the last time you sinned? You are technically still a sinner. Yet God does not see you through your record; he sees you through Christ, who is your representative and advocate. Romans 4:3 and Galatians 3:5-6 confirm the pattern: Abraham believed God and it was credited to him for righteousness. The credit is real, given in advance of completion.

That is justification. Sanctification is the ongoing process of God making real what he has declared. A new believer is like a child learning to walk — falls are expected; the question is whether they get up. 1 John 2:1 is the safety net: "If any man sins, we have an advocate with the Father, Jesus Christ the righteous." That sits alongside 1 John 3:6 and 3:9, which say those in Christ do not sin — meaning they do not practice sin, do not wallow in it, do not return to it as a way of life. The two are not in conflict: the Christian falls; the Christian gets up; the Christian is not defined by the fall.

Assurance is not a reward for sinlessness. As long as you cling to Christ, hate sin, and keep repenting — genuine repentance, not escape from punishment but sorrow that sin offended God and drove Christ to the cross — the declaration of righteousness stands. You are accepted in the beloved.

## Entry `canonical-017b`

- `question`: `Who are the people who claim to be Jews and are not, and what is the synagogue of Satan in Revelation 2:9?`
- `scripture_basis`: `Revelation 2:9; Revelation 3:9; Romans 2:28-29; Romans 9:6-8; Galatians 3:29; John 1:47-49; John 8:39-44`

- `secondary_support`: ``
- `tags`: `synagogue-of-Satan, Revelation-2, Revelation-3, Smyrna, Philadelphia, Jews, Israel, true-Jew, Romans-2, Romans-9, Galatians-3, John-1, John-8, Nathanael, Abraham, persecution, church-history, seven-churches`

### Answer

The phrase appears in exactly two places: Revelation 2:9, the letter to Smyrna, and Revelation 3:9, the letter to Philadelphia. Both say the same thing — "those who say they are Jews and are not, but are a synagogue of Satan." Reading this as a reference to ethnic Jewish people is to misread the book of Revelation, which is saturated with Old Testament Jewish terminology applied to the church age: Balaam, Jezebel, the synagogue itself — all Old Testament names and institutions applied to conditions within the Christian church.

The question is then: who is a Jew in the church age? Paul answers it directly. Romans 2:28-29: "He is not a Jew who is one outwardly... but he is a Jew who is one inwardly, and circumcision is that of the heart, in the spirit." Romans 9:6-8: "They are not all Israel who are of Israel." Galatians 3:29: "If you are Christ's, then you are Abraham's seed and heirs according to the promise." A true Jew is one who has received Jesus Christ as Savior and Lord.

John 1:47-49 shows the positive case. Jesus sees Nathanael approaching and says, "Behold, an Israelite indeed in whom there is no deceit" — and Nathanael confesses him as Son of God, King of Israel. That confession is what makes a true Israelite. John 8:39-44 shows the negative case at full length: Jesus tells the Jewish religious leaders that Abraham is their biological ancestor but not their spiritual father. "If you were Abraham's children, you would do the works of Abraham." They seek to kill him. Their father is the devil. Physical descent is not the issue; doing the works of Abraham — which includes receiving the Messiah — is.

The synagogue of Satan is therefore not a building or a formal organization. It is a disposition and a mindset. In the context of the seven churches, Smyrna is the persecuted church. Those who claim to be God's people — to hold the truth, to be in the covenant — but reject Christ and align themselves with those opposing the church are the synagogue of Satan. They claim to be Jews; they are not. The lie is the claim.
