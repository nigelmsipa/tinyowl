## Entry `ilk-026a`

- `question`: `Is there a rightful place for deliverance ministry within the church?`
- `scripture_basis`: `Romans 6:14-16; 2 Corinthians 5:17; Acts 19:11-16; Mark 1:21-26`
- `tags`: `deliverance, exorcism, demons, sanctification, Romans-6, Acts-19, Mark-1, sons-of-Sceva, spiritual-warfare`

### Answer

Deliverance has a place, but not in the form many charismatic and evangelical churches practice it, where a person confronts a demon directly, talks with it, and commands it out on the spot regardless of the individual's own spiritual state.

In a biblical sense, all real ministry is deliverance ministry. When a person surrenders to Christ, the power of sin is broken over that life. Romans 6 says sin no longer has dominion. The presence of sin also loses its grip, because in Christ the old things are passed away and all things become new (2 Corinthians 5:17). And when Christ returns, the presence of sin is finally removed from the universe altogether. That is the full scope of deliverance.

For the believer now, deliverance is a process of sanctification. It comes through communion with Christ, through prayer, through the anointing of elders and pastors, and through a daily walk in which the believer resists the devil and the devil flees. It is not a single dramatic moment that settles the whole matter.

There are cases in Scripture where demons must be confronted directly. Jesus did so in the synagogue at Capernaum and with the possessed man at Gadara. Paul lists casting out demons among the gifts of the Spirit. So the work itself is real.

But Acts 19 is a warning. The seven sons of Sceva took it upon themselves to use the name of Jesus as a formula, saying, "We exorcise you by Jesus whom Paul preaches." The evil spirit answered, "Jesus I know, and Paul I know, but who are you?" The man then overpowered them, and they fled naked and wounded. Their profession was exorcism, but their lives were not in order with the Lord, and the name of Jesus is not a tool to be wielded from the outside.

So do not look for a demon behind every stone. Do not make a profession of confronting devils. If the Lord places you in a situation where such a confrontation is necessary, be sure that you are prayed up and walking with Christ. Otherwise stay inside what God has actually called you to.

## Entry `ilk-026b`

- `question`: `What does buying and selling mean in Revelation 13 — literal or spiritual?`
- `scripture_basis`: `Revelation 13:11, 16-17; Proverbs 23:23`
- `tags`: `Revelation-13, mark-of-the-beast, seal-of-God, buying-and-selling, Sabbath, Sunday, United-States, Proverbs-23, end-times, little-things`

### Answer

It is both.

Revelation 13:16-17 says no one will be able to buy or sell unless they receive the mark of the beast in the right hand or in the forehead. That is literal. There will come a point when economic participation will be tied to the mark.

But the deeper issue is the truth. The final contest is over whether a person keeps the Sabbath, which is the seal of God, or knowingly keeps Sunday after being shown the truth, which is the mark of the beast. Proverbs 23:23 says, "Buy the truth, and sell it not; also wisdom, and instruction, and understanding."

So the two sides of buying and selling line up this way. The person who buys the truth and will not sell it comes under the death decree and cannot buy or sell. The person who sells the truth escapes the death decree and is able to buy and sell. One transaction is economic, the other is spiritual, and they are happening at the same time.

This comes in the time of the second beast of Revelation 13:11, the land beast, which in prophecy is the United States. The mark is not yet being enforced, but the issues that lead to it are already being drawn.

That is why the little decisions now matter. If a person is willing to compromise on the Sabbath, on honesty, on integrity in small things today, it will be much easier for that person to cave when life itself is on the line. But if a person has been standing for the Lord in small things, the preparation is already there for the larger test. The test is coming to everyone alive at that time, and no one escapes the decision.

## Entry `ilk-026c`

- `question`: `What do the moon under the woman's feet and the crown of 12 stars in Revelation 12 represent?`
- `scripture_basis`: `Revelation 12:1`
- `secondary_support`: `Ellen White, Acts of the Apostles, pages 16, 18`
- `tags`: `Revelation-12, woman, church, 12-patriarchs, 12-apostles, sun, moon, stars, Old-Testament, New-Testament, Christ`

### Answer

The woman of Revelation 12 represents the church. Before the child is born she is the Old Testament church; after the child is born she is the New Testament church. The child she brings forth is Christ.

The twelve stars on her head point to the twelve patriarchs who stood as representatives of Old Testament Israel and the twelve apostles who stood as representatives of the gospel church. Together they mark God's church across both testaments and across all ages of history. Ellen White makes the same point, writing that as the twelve patriarchs stood as representatives of Israel, so the twelve apostles stand as representatives of the gospel church, and that it was at the ordination of the twelve that the first step was taken in the organization of the church that was to carry on Christ's work after His departure.

The sun the woman is clothed with represents the fulfillment that came in Christ. The moon under her feet represents the Old Testament — the shadows and reflections that did not give their own light but reflected the light still to come. The Old Testament gave testimony to Christ, pointing forward to the greater light of His day.

So the picture is of one church, founded on the twelve patriarchs and the twelve apostles, resting on the witness of the Old Testament, and fully clothed at last in the light of Christ Himself.
