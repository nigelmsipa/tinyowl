### Answer

Submission to a husband does not extend to disobeying God. Ephesians 5:22 says "wives submit yourselves unto your own husbands as unto the Lord" — the qualifier matters. The submission is modeled on the wife's submission to the Lord, which means it cannot require what the Lord himself forbids. There is a ceiling on spousal authority, and "obey God rather than man" is that ceiling.

On guilt and salvation: she should not feel condemned. Her heart is right — she wants to tithe and would if she could. If she has no control whatsoever over the money, God will hold the husband accountable, not her. Accountability tracks with control.

The practical guidance is to pay tithe on what she does control. Both spouses receive separate social security checks; her check is hers. If he gives her money for groceries or household expenses, she can tithe on what passes through her hands. Start there and trust Malachi 3: "Prove me now herewith — I will open the windows of heaven and pour out a blessing till you have no room to receive it." Bohr illustrates with the widow of Zarephath, who gave Elijah the last of her oil and flour first, and the jar did not run out until rain came. The test of faith has to be made before the balance sheet balances — that is the whole point.

She should keep speaking to her husband, persistently and with grace. One case was shared where a wife's steady witness over several months wore down a resistant husband; when they began tithing together they saw clear blessings and he eventually came to the church. The principle of the persistent widow applies: consistent, loving appeal, not nagging but earnest witness, often accomplishes what a single confrontation cannot.

## Entry `canonical-018b`

- `question`: `In Revelation 17:10, five heads have fallen, one is, and the other has not yet come. If the fifth head is papal Rome and John's vision was written before papal Rome, how is John viewing this after the fifth head has already fallen?`
- `scripture_basis`: `Revelation 17:10; Revelation 17:1-2`
- `source_scripture_usage`: `Revelation 17:10 is read directly. Revelation 17:1-2 is read directly by Bohr. Revelation 12 is referenced for the parallel between the dragon's waters and the harlot's waters. Daniel 2 and Daniel 7 are named in the layered expansion pattern but not opened or read.`
- `secondary_support`: ``
- `tags`: `Revelation-17, seven-heads, seven-kings, five-fallen, papal-Rome, Babylon, Medo-Persia, Greece, Rome, deadly-wound, harlot, many-waters, Revelation-12, Daniel-2, Daniel-7, recapitulation, end-time, prophecy-structure`

### Answer

The answer is in the vantage point of the vision. Revelation 17:1-2 opens with one of the seven bowl angels saying to John, "Come, I will show you the judgment of the great harlot who sits on many waters." John is not viewing this from his own time in the first century. He is being transported to the end time — to the period when the papacy has recovered from its deadly wound and the multitudes, nations, and tongues are once again supporting her.

From that end-time vantage point, five have already fallen: Babylon, Medo-Persia, Greece, pagan Rome, and papal Rome in its first phase (the phase that ended with the deadly wound). The sixth — the papacy in its wounded and diminished state — "is" in the flow of history between the wound and the healing. The seventh, which "has not yet come" and "must continue a short time," is the fully healed papacy of the final period. John is not confused about chronology; he is simply standing at the end and looking back.

Revelation is not in chronological order. It moves in cycles — forward to the end, then back to lay the frame of reference, then forward again. To understand Revelation 17, Bohr argues you must work the layered structure: Daniel 2 is the foundation; Daniel 7 expands it; Revelation 12 expands further; Revelation 13 builds on 12; Revelation 17 is the final and fullest expansion. The waters the harlot sits on in Revelation 17 parallel the dragon's flood in Revelation 12 — the same persecuting power at different stages of history. Only when you see that building pattern does the placement of "five have fallen" make sense — because you know which sequence of empires has already run its course before the end-time scene.
