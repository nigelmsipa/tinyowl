## Entry `ilk-019a`

- `question`: `How should you understand Ezekiel 39's seven years of burning weapons and seven months of burying the dead in relation to the final destruction of the wicked?`
- `scripture_basis`: `Ezekiel 39; Joel 3; Jeremiah 25; Revelation 14; Revelation 19:17-21; Revelation 20:7-9`
- `tags`: `Ezekiel-39, Gog-and-Magog, Revelation, typology, end-times, wicked, Jeremiah-25, Joel-3`

### Answer

Do not force every local detail in Ezekiel 39 into the final global destruction scene.

The basic principle is that Revelation universalizes what many Old Testament passages first described in local terms for literal Israel. Joel speaks of the valley of Jehoshaphat near Jerusalem, but Revelation 14 speaks of the harvest of the whole earth. Ezekiel speaks of Gog and Magog in a local prophetic setting, but Revelation 20 globalizes Gog and Magog to the four corners of the earth.

So not every detail carries over one-for-one. The general pattern carries over, but the local features do not always remain literal in the final application. So the seven years of burning weapons and the seven months of burying bodies are not pressed as end-time chronologies that must fit after the second coming.

Jeremiah 25 also helps control the interpretation, because it describes the earth lying desolate with the bodies of the wicked spread upon it. That does not fit the idea of the saints spending months burying corpses during the millennial desolation.

So let Revelation supply the fuller end-time framework, and let Ezekiel support it at the level of principle and imagery. If you try to make every detail line up mechanically, you will confuse yourself instead of clarifying prophecy.

## Entry `ilk-019b`

- `question`: `How will God judge babies and little children who die before reaching the age of accountability?`
- `scripture_basis`: `Acts 4:12; 1 John 3:4; James 4:17`
- `secondary_support`: `Faith and Works, p. 56; Heaven, p. 45`
- `tags`: `babies, children, heaven, accountability, salvation, sin, Acts-4, James-4`

### Answer

If babies and little children are saved, they are saved through the merits of Jesus Christ, not apart from Him.

Acts 4 settles the foundation: there is no salvation in any other name. So even when a child has not reached moral accountability, no one enters heaven except through Christ.

First John 3:4 and James 4:17 are then used to explain why accountability matters. Sin is the transgression of the law, and James says, `to him that knoweth to do good, and doeth it not, to him it is sin.` That means guilt in the fullest sense involves knowledgeable, responsible participation in evil. A baby inherits a fallen nature, but has not yet knowingly chosen rebellion in the same way.

That is why the supporting material can speak of infants in the resurrection, including some whose mothers are not there. So there will be little children in heaven, and their presence there will still be entirely through Christ's grace.

At the same time, do not go beyond what has been clearly revealed. God has not answered every detail now. But this much is safe: He is just, He is merciful, He is not trying to keep anyone out who can be saved, and every judgment He makes will finally be seen as right.
