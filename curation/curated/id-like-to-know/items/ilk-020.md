## Entry `ilk-020a`

- `question`: `If Galatians 5 says there is no law against the fruit of the Spirit, why would anyone still need to keep the Sabbath or the rest of God's law?`
- `scripture_basis`: `Galatians 5:14-25; Romans 3:19; Romans 7:1-6; James 2:12; 1 John 2:3-6`
- `tags`: `Galatians-5, Sabbath, law, fruit-of-the-Spirit, Holy-Spirit, obedience, Romans-3, 1-John-2`

### Answer

The fruit of the Spirit does not cancel God's law. It fulfills the life that God's law describes.

Paul is not saying in Galatians 5 that spiritual people no longer need the commandments. He is saying that when you are led by the Spirit, the law is no longer standing over you as a condemning power, because you are no longer living in rebellion against it.

Romans 3 helps explain what it means to be under the law. Those who are under the law are guilty before God because the law is speaking against their disobedience. But when the Spirit leads you into obedience, the law is not condemning you. That is why Paul can say that against the fruit of the Spirit there is no law.

There is no contradiction here with the Sabbath or the rest of the commandments. If you love God and your neighbor, you are not abolishing the law. You are living it out. Love is the first fruit named in Galatians 5, and love is exactly what the commandments require.

So do not set the Spirit against the law. The Holy Spirit wrote the law, produces the fruit of the Spirit, and leads God's people into harmony with all His commandments, including the Sabbath.

## Entry `ilk-020b`

- `question`: `What is the difference between a pastor and a church elder, and is their ordination different?`
- `scripture_basis`: `1 Timothy 3:1; Titus 1; 1 Peter 5:1-4`
- `tags`: `pastor, elder, bishop, overseer, ordination, church-leadership, 1-Timothy-3, Titus-1`

### Answer

Biblically, the role of pastor, elder, and overseer belongs to the same line of church leadership, even though the sphere of service may differ.

The New Testament language overlaps. A bishop or overseer is the same basic office of spiritual leadership that is elsewhere described with elder language. That is why the passages read at pastoral ordinations are usually the same kinds of passages that describe elders and overseers.

So the main difference here is not one of spiritual rank, but one of scope. A local elder serves a particular congregation. A pastor is authorized to serve more broadly, often over a district or multiple churches.

That means you should not imagine the pastor as belonging to a different spiritual species from the elder. The pastor is not a higher-order Christian. Both are called to shepherd, guide, care, and minister.

So yes, a pastor is an elder in the biblical sense, and the ordination difference is mainly functional and organizational, not a difference in sacred essence.

## Entry `ilk-020c`

- `question`: `What is the sea of glass in Revelation, and will there be baptisms in it?`
- `scripture_basis`: `Revelation 4:6; Revelation 15:2`
- `secondary_support`: `The Great Controversy, p. 648`
- `tags`: `sea-of-glass, Revelation-4, Revelation-15, heaven, crystal-sea, baptism, throne-of-God`

### Answer

The sea of glass is something real before the throne of God, but Scripture does not tell you enough to describe its material with precision.

Revelation 4 says it is `like crystal,` and Revelation 15 says it is `mingled with fire.` That suggests something clear, calm, radiant, and reflecting the glory of God. The supporting material explains that the fiery appearance comes from the glory of God shining upon it.

It may be real water from the river of life spread out before the throne in such purity and stillness that it looks like glass. But that remains an inference from the imagery, not a direct statement of the text.

As for baptism, no. There will be no baptisms in the sea of glass, because there will be no more sin there. Baptism belongs to the present order where repentance, cleansing, and public identification with Christ are still needed.

So the sea of glass is part of heaven's glory, not a future baptismal pool.
