### Answer

Deliverance ministry has a legitimate place in the church, but the term requires careful definition. In its broadest sense all ministry is deliverance ministry: when a person surrenders to Christ, Romans 6:14-16 declares that sin no longer has dominion, and 2 Corinthians 5:17 confirms that former things pass away — the power of sin is broken. This progressive freedom from sin's power, and eventually its presence, is the biblical shape of deliverance.

The charismatic form — directly confronting and conversing with demons in others — does not match the biblical pattern. Acts 19:11-16 is instructive: God worked extraordinary miracles through Paul, but when the seven sons of Sceva attempted to cast out demons using Jesus' name without a personal relationship with him, the evil spirit answered "Jesus I know, and Paul I know — but who are you?" and overpowered them. The lesson is not that exorcism never occurs but that it requires one's own life to be in order with the Lord and a specific assignment from Christ.

Mark 1:21-27 shows Jesus casting out an unclean spirit with direct authority. The biblical model for ordinary believers is not seeking out demonic confrontations. Through prayer, communion, the anointing of elders, and ongoing relationship with Christ the enemy flees. Deliverance comes through sanctification, not through circuit-riding demon-chasing.

## Entry `canonical-021b`

- `question`: `What does buying and selling mean in Revelation 13:17 — is it literal or spiritual?`
- `scripture_basis`: `Revelation 13:16-17; Proverbs 23:23`
- `source_scripture_usage`: `Revelation 13:16-17 is read directly by Murray/Bohr. Revelation 13:11 is cited by verse to identify the land beast as the context for the buying-and-selling restriction. Proverbs 23:23 is quoted directly by Bohr — "Buy the truth and sell it not. Also wisdom and instruction and understanding" — and serves as the hermeneutical key that unlocks the both/and answer.`
- `secondary_support`: ``
- `tags`: `buying-and-selling, Revelation-13, mark-of-the-beast, seal-of-God, Sabbath, Sunday, Proverbs-23, land-beast, United-States-in-prophecy, literal-spiritual, end-times, truth`

### Answer

Revelation 13:16-17 states plainly that no one may buy or sell except those who bear the mark of the beast. The restriction is literal: commerce will be closed to those who refuse the mark. But the question of why — the spiritual condition that triggers the literal consequence — is what Proverbs 23:23 unlocks.

"Buy the truth and sell it not," Proverbs says. In the final crisis the truth at stake is the Sabbath — God's seal — versus the mark of the beast, which is knowingly keeping Sunday when the Sabbath is the day of rest. Those who buy the truth embrace God's seal and accept the economic death decree that follows. Those who sell the truth accept the mark and gain commercial access. As Bohr puts it: you can buy the truth and be under a death decree, or you can sell the truth and be spared the death decree.

The answer is therefore not either/or — it is both/and. The commercial restriction is real and literal. The reason for it is spiritual. The two levels are inseparable: the literal buying and selling maps directly onto a spiritual transaction over the truth.

## Entry `canonical-021c`

- `question`: `Who is the woman clothed with the sun in Revelation 12, and what do the moon and the 12 stars on her head represent?`
- `scripture_basis`: `Revelation 12:1-2`
- `source_scripture_usage`: `Revelation 12:1-2 is read directly by Murray. The identification of "woman = church" is stated as a hermeneutical principle without a supporting verse being cited. The identification of the child as Christ is asserted without citing a verse (Revelation 12:5 is not opened). Both Ellen White quotations are read directly by Bohr.`
- `secondary_support`: `Ellen G. White, Acts of the Apostles, page 16; Acts of the Apostles, page 18`
- `tags`: `Revelation-12, woman-clothed-with-sun, church, 12-stars, 12-patriarchs, 12-apostles, Old-Testament-church, New-Testament-church, sun-moon-stars, typology, Ellen-White, Acts-of-the-Apostles`

### Answer

In Revelation 12:1-2 a woman appears clothed with the sun, the moon under her feet, and a crown of twelve stars — she is in labor. The woman in Revelation represents the church. A pregnant church births only one thing: Christ.

The twelve stars represent the twelve foundational figures of God's people across both Testaments — the twelve patriarchs who founded the Old Testament church, and the twelve apostles who founded the New Testament church. The number twelve spans all of redemptive history. Ellen White confirms this at Acts of the Apostles page 16: "As in the Old Testament, the twelve patriarchs stood as representatives of Israel, so the twelve apostles stand as representatives of the gospel church." And at page 18: "It was at the ordination of the twelve that the first step was taken in the organization of the church that after Christ's departure was to carry on his work on the earth."

The sun the woman wears represents the fulfillment of the Old Testament — Christ as the greater light. The moon under her feet represents the Old Testament itself, a reflected light whose shadows and types pointed forward to the sun. The Old Testament bore witness to the light; the New Testament is that light's arrival. Jesus Christ is the chief cornerstone on which both the twelve patriarchs and the twelve apostles rest.
