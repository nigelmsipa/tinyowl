## Entry `ilk-006a`

- `question`: `Did Jesus ever get sick?`
- `scripture_basis`: `Exodus 15:26; Luke 2:39-40; Hebrews 4:15`
- `tags`: `Jesus, sickness, health, temperance, humanity, sinlessness`

### Answer

Scripture does not directly say that Jesus was ever sick, so do not claim a level of certainty the text itself does not give. Even so, the reasoning in this answer leans strongly toward `probably not`.

Jesus is presented as living in full harmony with God's law, including the principles that govern the body. Hebrews 4:15 says He was tempted in all points as we are, yet without sin. Luke 2:39-40 also presents Him as a child who grew strong. The broader biblical principle is that obedience to God's ways protects life and health rather than destroying them.

The answer also distinguishes sickness from weakness. Jesus became physically weak through fasting in the wilderness and through the agony of Gethsemane, but that is not the same thing as becoming sick through disorder, transgression, or bodily breakdown.

So the careful answer is this: the Bible does not explicitly say that Jesus never got sick, but the reasoning used here is that He lived so fully in harmony with God's principles that sickness does not appear to fit the picture Scripture gives of His life.

## Entry `ilk-006b`

- `question`: `How should you answer arguments for abortion in extreme circumstances such as danger to the mother, rape, or incest?`
- `scripture_basis`: `Genesis 35:16-18`
- `tags`: `abortion, sanctity-of-life, rape, incest, maternal-danger, ethics, unborn`

### Answer

Begin with the sanctity of life. God is the giver of life, and human beings do not possess an automatic right to take innocent life, including unborn life.

This approach does not create a list of biblical exceptions and then treat abortion as justified whenever a hard case appears. Instead, it presses the opposite question: if the mother's life is in danger, can God preserve both mother and child if He chooses? Rachel's death in childbirth is brought in to show that childbirth can involve real mortal danger, but the conclusion drawn from that is not that human beings should seize authority over life and death.

The answer also refuses the logic that extreme circumstances make the unborn child less human or less worthy of protection. That is why the discussion pushes back against reasoning based on disability, trauma, or abnormal conditions. The value of a human life does not collapse because the surrounding circumstances are painful or tragic.

So the answer is to leave life in God's hands rather than take it into your own. This source frames the issue around the sanctity of life and rejects the idea that human beings should decide that an unborn child may be deliberately killed because the circumstances are severe.

## Entry `ilk-006c`

- `question`: `What should you do when demons pretend to be dead loved ones or beings from other worlds?`
- `scripture_basis`: `Matthew 4:8-10; James 4:7; Ecclesiastes 9:5`
- `secondary_support`: `Early Writings`
- `tags`: `demons, dead-loved-ones, deception, occult, immortality-of-the-soul, resistance, last-days`

### Answer

Resist the devil in the name of Jesus, and answer the deception with Scripture instead of emotion.

James 4:7 supplies the basic command: `Resist the devil, and he will flee from you.` Ecclesiastes 9:5 gives the doctrinal guardrail: `the dead know not any thing.` So when a supposed dead relative appears, do not treat the experience as evidence. Treat it as a satanic counterfeit and rebuke it in Christ's name.

The answer also warns that preparation begins before the crisis. If you feed the mind on occult material, sentimental falsehoods about the dead, or emotionally driven ideas about ongoing contact with departed loved ones, you are training yourself for deception. Satan's counterfeit is described here as emotionally powerful, not cartoonishly obvious.

That is why feelings cannot be trusted above revelation. A spirit may look right, sound right, and appeal directly to grief and affection. But God's people are to answer with `Thus saith the Lord`, not with their senses. The living know they will die. The dead know nothing. Stand there, resist the devil, and refuse the experience in the name of Jesus.

## Entry `ilk-006d`

- `question`: `Who will the righteous reign over during the thousand years?`
- `scripture_basis`: `Revelation 20:4-6; 1 Corinthians 6:1-3`
- `tags`: `millennium, thousand-years, reign, judgment, Satan, angels, wicked`

### Answer

The righteous reign with Christ during the thousand years by sharing in His work of judgment.

This is not presented here as the redeemed ruling earthly nations in the usual political sense. Christ is the one who reigns, and His people sit with Him in that reign as participants in judgment. First Corinthians 6 says the saints will judge the world and will judge angels. That gives the meaning of reigning during the millennium.

So the reign is judicial. The redeemed review God's judgments regarding the lost, Satan, and the fallen angels. They see why certain people are absent, why sentences are just, and how God's decisions have been perfectly righteous.

In that sense, the saints reign over the wicked and over fallen angels by participating with Christ in the judgment that determines and confirms their final sentence.
