## Entry `canonical-008a`

- `question`: `If the ten northern tribes lost their pure lineage in the Assyrian captivity, how could Elizabeth be identified as from the tribe of Levi and Anna as from the tribe of Asher?`
- `scripture_basis`: ``
- `source_scripture_usage`: `No passage is exposited. The answer is a historical/genealogical argument, with Josiah's reformation, the Assyrian captivity (723 BC), and Paul's Benjamite identity referenced by way of summary.`
- `secondary_support`: `Josiah's reformation (2 Kings 23:1-25), Paul's Benjamite identity (Romans 11:1, Philippians 3:5)`
- `tags`: `lost-tribes, Assyrian-captivity, Elizabeth, Anna, Levi, Asher, Samaritans, Josiah-reformation, genealogy, remnant`

### Answer

Not every Israelite in the northern tribes lost their identity. A good portion intermarried with the Assyrians and the surrounding pagan nations — that is the origin of the Samaritans — but a remnant in each tribe remained faithful and kept the line. Josiah's reformation shows it clearly: when he reached into the territory of the northern tribes, there were people there who returned to the Lord.

Levi is the easiest case, because the tribe of Levi was tied to the sanctuary service in Judah. Many Levites returned with the post-exilic community to Jerusalem, so Elizabeth's Levitical descent in the New Testament is not a surprise. Anna from the tribe of Asher is the harder case, but the same principle applies: some remained, kept records, and preserved their tribal identity through the captivity and after. By the New Testament period, tribal identity also does not necessarily imply pure blood. Paul calls himself a Benjamite while living in Gentile territory. Intermarriage happened throughout the Old Testament and people still counted as Israelites. So Elizabeth and Anna being named from their tribes reflects a preserved line, not a claim of genetic purity across seven centuries.

## Entry `canonical-008b`

- `question`: `How can God sanction one nation attacking and killing another, as with Israel and the Moabites in 2 Kings 3?`
- `scripture_basis`: `Genesis 15:16; Exodus 23:27-30; Deuteronomy 2:9, 19; Daniel 5:27`
- `source_scripture_usage`: `Genesis 15 is walked through in summary — the promise to Abraham delayed because "the iniquity of the Amorite is not yet full." The "weighed in the balances" of Daniel 5 is referenced. No single verse is opened verbatim.`
- `secondary_support`: `Ellen G. White on national apostasy and national ruin`
- `tags`: `holy-war, Moab, Canaan, Amorites, Genesis-15, iniquity-full, Exodus-23, hornet, Deuteronomy-2, unpardonable-sin, national-apostasy, flood, Sodom, Daniel-5, free-will`

### Answer

God does not destroy a nation arbitrarily. He destroys it when it has gone beyond the point of no return, and only He knows when that line is crossed.

Genesis 15 sets the pattern. God promised Abraham the land but delayed its possession by four hundred years "because the iniquity of the Amorite is not yet full." Probation, in effect, had not closed on them. By the time of Moses, several generations later, that fullness had been reached. That is when the judgment falls. The same principle governs the Flood, Sodom and Gomorrah, and the final destruction of the wicked at Christ's coming. If destroying the wicked at the end is consistent with divine justice — and Scripture says it is — then the Old Testament judgments follow the same logic.

God's preferred method was not military conquest. Exodus 23 promised to drive the Canaanites out little by little through the hornet and the wild beasts of the land. That plan required faith. Israel's unbelief forced the transition to war; God still worked through them at the level they were on. So the warfare is not a picture of God's first will but of God meeting His people in their weakness while still protecting His name among the nations. In that cultural world, whichever god's people won was believed to be the stronger god, so when Israel was mocked and attacked, God's name itself was at stake.

Moab is a good example of timing. When Israel first passed through on the way to the Jordan, God told Moses not to fight Moab, Edom, or Ammon — they were kin through Lot and Abraham, and their cup was not yet full. Later those same nations hardened further, and only then do we see the wars of 2 Kings 3 and others.

Nations can commit the unpardonable sin, just as individuals can. Babylon was told, "You have been weighed in the balances and found wanting." At the end, the wicked themselves will ask to be destroyed because they cannot stand to live in the presence of the God they have learned to despise. God is simply giving them the full result of the choice they have been making. That is the frame for Old Testament holy war: divine patience first, judgment only when the line has been crossed, and the choice always respected.

## Entry `canonical-008c`

- `question`: `If wars seem fewer now than in Old Testament times, what did Jesus mean in Matthew 24 about wars and rumors of wars as a sign of the end?`
- `scripture_basis`: `Matthew 24:4-8`
- `source_scripture_usage`: `Matthew 24 is named and paraphrased, not opened verse by verse. The argument is about the convergence and scale of signs, not an exposition of a single passage.`
- `secondary_support`: `Ellen G. White, The Great Controversy, pages 589-590, on increasing frequency and severity of disasters`
- `tags`: `Matthew-24, signs-of-the-end, wars, false-Christs, deceptions, global-war, perfect-storm, papacy, United-States, convergence, Revelation`

### Answer

The sign is not that there are wars for the first time. Wars have always existed. What Jesus is describing is a convergence — wars on a worldwide scale, more bloody, more repetitive, combined with all the other signs.

Matthew 24 does not begin with wars. It begins with deception — false Christs, counterfeit spiritual manifestations, angels pretending to be Christ. When those deceptions rise, the other signs — wars, pestilences, earthquakes — are already being fulfilled, but now in greater intensity and frequency. The First and Second World Wars are a pattern the ancient world never saw. Old Testament wars were regional, largely confined to Asia and later Europe. Today the contest is global, with the major powers drawing the nations into their alignment.

And Matthew 24 is not the only list. Revelation adds the ascent of the United States in global power, the healing of the wound of the papacy, and more. Ellen White observes in The Great Controversy (pages 589-590) that the disasters God allows will become more frequent and more severe as the end nears. It is the accumulation — wars, natural disasters, social unrest, deceptions, political and religious realignments — all intensifying at the same time that constitutes the sign. That is the perfect storm Jesus is pointing at, not any single war in isolation.

## Entry `canonical-008d`

- `question`: `Why did Ellen White call the 1888 message "the third angel's message in verity," and if it had been accepted at Minneapolis would it have brought the latter rain and finished the work?`
- `scripture_basis`: `Revelation 14:12`
- `source_scripture_usage`: `Revelation 14:12 is quoted directly as the anchor — "Here is the patience of the saints; here are they that keep the commandments of God and the faith of Jesus." The bulk of the answer runs through Ellen White on 1888 rather than through additional Scripture.`
- `secondary_support`: `Ellen G. White, Testimonies to Ministers, pages 91-92; Review and Herald, June 4, 1895`
- `tags`: `1888, Minneapolis, third-angel, Jones-and-Waggoner, law-in-Galatians, righteousness-by-faith, justification, sanctification, Revelation-14-12, latter-rain, imputed-righteousness, imparted-righteousness`

### Answer

Yes. Had the message been received, it would have brought the latter rain and finished the work.

The reason Ellen White called the 1888 message the third angel's message in verity is that it restored the missing half of Revelation 14:12. The third angel ends with, "Here is the patience of the saints; here are they that keep the commandments of God and the faith of Jesus." Adventism had dwelt heavily on the commandments of God, but had not preached the faith of Jesus with the same weight. Salvation had been drifting toward works. At Minneapolis, the older pioneers accused Jones and Waggoner of undermining the law. Ellen White's response was the opposite: they were giving the law its right place by putting the faith of Jesus back where it belonged, because it is through the faith of Jesus that the commandments of God are kept.

She captured the message in Testimonies to Ministers pages 91-92: the 1888 message presented justification through faith in the Surety, Christ as the Surety, inviting people to receive the righteousness of Christ — a justification that is made manifest in obedience to all the commandments of God. And in Review and Herald, June 4, 1895, she wrote: "Righteousness within is testified by righteousness without. The righteousness by which we are justified is imputed. The righteousness by which we are sanctified is imparted. The first is our title to heaven. The second is our fitness for heaven." You do not go to heaven with only one. The 1888 message held both together. Rejecting it did not simply delay a revival; it delayed the third angel's message in its fullness and with it the latter rain.
