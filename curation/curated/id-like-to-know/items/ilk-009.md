## Entry `ilk-009a`

- `question`: `Why did angels choose individually whether to follow Christ or Satan, while the human race suffers from Adam's sin and inherited sinful nature?`
- `scripture_basis`: `Romans 5:12-19; Matthew 22:30`
- `secondary_support`: `Steps to Christ`
- `tags`: `Adam, angels, hereditary-sin, original-sin, free-choice, redemption, inheritance`

### Answer

The difference is tied to inheritance. Angels do not procreate, so they do not pass a fallen nature from one generation to another. Human beings do.

The angels each made an individual choice to remain loyal or to follow Satan, but humanity also suffers the hereditary consequences of Adam's fall. Romans is the background for that contrast. In Adam, the race falls under sin's ruin; in Christ, the race is given the way back.

So the human problem is not just that each person eventually chooses sin individually. It is also that a sinful nature is inherited within the race. That inherited condition is what the source says is absent in the case of angels, because there is no angelic propagation passing corruption forward.

But that does not leave humanity without hope or excuse. Christ entered the problem because He loved His creation and had already provided the plan of redemption. Through His grace, repentance, and the power of the Holy Spirit, the hereditary pull of sin does not have to reign unchecked. The same one-man logic that helps explain the fall also explains the rescue: what was ruined through one man is opened again through one man.

So the answer is not that God treated angels and humans unfairly. The answer is that the structure of angelic life and human life is different, and Christ has made full provision for human redemption within the very condition Adam brought upon the race.

## Entry `ilk-009b`

- `question`: `If God already knows everything about me, why is there an investigative judgment? Who is being investigated, and for whose sake is it done?`
- `scripture_basis`: `Ecclesiastes 12:13-14; 1 Corinthians 4:9; 1 Corinthians 6:1-3; Revelation 17:8`
- `secondary_support`: `The Great Controversy, p. 483`
- `tags`: `investigative-judgment, pre-advent-judgment, transparency, books, millennium, judgment, universe`

### Answer

God does not investigate because He lacks information. He investigates so the universe can see that every judgment He makes is righteous, transparent, and fully justified.

Before the second coming, the judgment focuses on those who profess Christ. Christ is coming to claim His people, so those cases are reviewed before He comes. The lost are not taken to heaven at the second coming, so their full case is not opened in the same way until later. During the millennium, the redeemed review the cases of the wicked. After the thousand years, the wicked themselves are shown why they are lost before final sentence is executed.

Ecclesiastes says God will bring every work into judgment, including every secret thing. That does not mean God is discovering hidden facts for the first time. It means those facts are being brought into the open. The judgment reveals what God already knows so that angels, redeemed human beings, and finally the lost themselves can see that He has acted without arbitrariness.

That is why the source compares judgment to the ordinary structure of jurisprudence: investigation, sentence, then execution. God follows that moral order, not because He needs procedural help, but because His government is open and just. First Corinthians 4 says the apostles are a spectacle to the universe, to angels, and to men. First Corinthians 6 says the saints will judge the world and angels. So the judgment is cosmic in scope.

This is also why the investigative judgment is presented as good news for believers. If Christ is your advocate, you have nothing to fear. The judgment is not God hunting for a reason to exclude the faithful. It is God demonstrating before the universe that His saving and condemning decisions are perfectly right.
