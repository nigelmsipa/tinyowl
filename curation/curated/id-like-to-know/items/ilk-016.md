## Entry `ilk-016a`

- `question`: `Are you born a sinner, and if not, what did Christ redeem you from?`
- `scripture_basis`: `1 John 3:4; James 4:17; Psalm 51:5; 1 John 1:8-9; Romans 8:1`
- `secondary_support`: `Selected Messages, book 1, on 1 John 3:4 as the definition of sin`
- `tags`: `sin, sinful-nature, sinner, Psalm-51, 1-John-3, Romans-8, accountability, Christ`

### Answer

You are born with a fallen, sinful nature, but sin in the strict biblical sense is the transgression of the law.

First John 3:4 is the key text. Sin is not defined first as mere fallen inheritance, weakness, or infirmity. Sin is lawbreaking. James 4:17 fits that same pattern, because refusing known duty is still transgression, even if it is sin by omission.

Psalm 51:5 explains the other side of the question. You do inherit a fallen condition from Adam's race. Selfishness, weakness, and a bent toward evil are present before mature moral judgment is formed. But possessing a fallen nature is not the same thing as consciously participating in sin.

So what did Christ redeem you from? He came to save you both from the fallen condition into which you are born and from the actual practice of sin. That is why 1 John 1:9 does not stop at forgiveness. Christ forgives and cleanses. Romans 8 then opens the hope of victory through life in the Spirit.

So do not say that fallen nature is harmless. It is not. But do not collapse fallen nature and conscious transgression into the exact same thing either. Christ came to deal with the whole ruin of sin: inherited brokenness, practiced rebellion, guilt, and the need for cleansing power.

## Entry `ilk-016b`

- `question`: `Will a literal third temple be rebuilt in Jerusalem as part of end-time prophecy, and does physical Israel still hold that central prophetic role?`
- `scripture_basis`: `Matthew 23:38; John 4:21-24; 1 Corinthians 3:16; Ephesians 2:19-22; 1 Peter 2:5; Hebrews 8:1-5; 2 Thessalonians 2:3-4`
- `tags`: `third-temple, Jerusalem, Israel, heavenly-sanctuary, church, spiritual-temple, end-times`

### Answer

No. Scripture is not pointing here to a rebuilt third temple in Jerusalem as a necessary center of the end-time scenario.

The basic reason is that in the New Testament the temple is no longer treated as the decisive earthly building it once was. Jesus said in Matthew 23, `Your house is left unto you desolate.` After His rejection, the old temple order lost its central significance because the true presence of God had been rejected.

From there the focus shifts. John 4 says that worship is no longer tied to a sacred geographic site in the old way, but to worship in spirit and in truth. Paul says the church is God's temple, and both Ephesians 2 and 1 Peter 2 describe believers as a spiritual house built together for God. Hebrews then directs the eyes upward to Christ ministering in the heavenly sanctuary.

So 2 Thessalonians 2 is not a prediction that some future political antichrist must sit inside a rebuilt Jerusalem shrine. The temple language there is read in light of the New Testament pattern, where the church and the heavenly sanctuary have taken interpretive priority over an earthly structure.

So do not fix your hopes on a third temple in old Jerusalem. Fix your eyes on Christ in the heavenly sanctuary and on the New Jerusalem that is to come.
