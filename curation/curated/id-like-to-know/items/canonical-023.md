### Answer

A trial has three stages: investigation of the evidence, verdict or sentence, and execution of the sentence. God’s judgment follows that biblical pattern. He does not simply declare someone saved or lost; there is a universe to convince that God has been right in every case, and that must happen before the second coming.

The judgment is future. Revelation 14:7 declares, “fear God and give glory to him, for the hour of his judgment has come” — and right after that message you see Jesus coming in the second coming. The judgment did not take place in New Testament times. Paul in Acts 17:31 says, “because he has appointed a day on which he will judge the world in righteousness by the man whom he has ordained. He has given assurance of this to all by raising him from the dead.” So there is a day when he will judge the world, and that day had not yet arrived in the apostles’ day.

Ellen White tells us that judgment would take place 300 years after Luther. The sanctuary message brings the picture of how and when judgment happens, and Daniel helps us understand where, why, and when.

The Bible is full of the concept of investigative judgment. It begins in the Garden of Eden: after Adam and Eve sinned, God comes and says, “Where are you?” He knew where Adam was — he is calling him to the judgment seat. “Have you eaten from the tree?” The examination is being made. Then God pronounces the sentence. The Tower of Babel: God says, “let’s go down and see if they have gone too far.” God knew, but he is teaching us the way judgment is performed. Genesis 18: Abraham intercedes, “Will not the judge of the earth do what is right?” God says, “I’m going to go down and see if the cities have gone too far.” Once again, an investigation of the case.

In the New Testament, the man who came to the party without the wedding garment, the good and bad fish, the wheat and tares, the wise and foolish virgins, and the forgiveness that can be revoked all illustrate that judgment examines works to prove the reality of commitment.

The judgment takes place in three stages. First, the pre‑advent investigative judgment — only those who claim the name of Jesus, who claim to have the righteousness of Christ, are examined before the second coming. Jesus must determine whom he has a right to take to heaven before he comes. Second, the millennial judgment — all the wicked are dead on earth, Satan and his angels are bound, and God proves to the righteous in heaven that he was correct in leaving everyone behind. Third, after the millennium, God opens the books in panoramic view and shows the wicked why they are lost, including the full history of Satan and his angels. When it is over, every knee bows and every tongue confesses that God did it right. Only at that moment can God destroy sin and sinners.

## Entry `canonical-023b`

- `question`: `Concerning Revelation 1:7 — “every eye will see him, even those that pierced him.” We believe those who crucified Christ will be resurrected to see his coming. When they receive the second death, will it actually be their third death? This verse says “every eye will see him” — will the righteous dead see Jesus approaching with the clouds, or only once he arrives and is in the clouds?`

### Answer

The Bible does not speak of a third death. It speaks of first death — physical death, which everyone will suffer except the end‑time generation — and second death, which is everlasting death. Physical death is not eternal. In the case of the second coming, several groups must be kept in mind. Those who are alive will see Jesus come in the clouds. There is also a specialized group of dead — those who pierced him — who will resurrect. And there is a group of righteous people — those who died in the faith of the third angel’s message — who resurrect at the voice of God before Jesus even begins his second coming. Then when Jesus is above the earth, the other righteous dead resurrect. They are not going to see the entire second coming from beginning to end.

In short, this special group of wicked people who pierced Christ will have three deaths in sequence: they died before the second coming, they resurrect in the special resurrection, they die again, and then after the panoramic view they suffer the second death. Even though we might say technically it is a third death in sequence, in the biblical definition it is second death because it is death from which there will be no resurrection.

Regarding whether they will see Jesus approaching — they will see him when Jesus is about to begin his second coming. Ellen White makes it clear this special group resurrects under the seventh plague, at the voice of God. Then Jesus begins his second coming. Christ told them prophetically what they would see: according to Matthew 26:64, Jesus said to Caiaphas, “From now on I tell you that you will see the Son of Man coming on the clouds of heaven with power and glory.” So yes, they will see him approach and realize that Christ was indeed the Messiah, the King of kings.
