## Entry `ilk-024a`

- `question`: `Does 1 Thessalonians 5:23 teach that a human being is made of three separable parts that can function independently?`
- `scripture_basis`: `1 Thessalonians 5:23`
- `tags`: `body, soul, spirit, human-nature, wholistic-anthropology, 1-Thessalonians-5`

### Answer

No. First Thessalonians 5:23 does not teach that the body, soul, and spirit are three independent parts that can each function on their own.

The point is that God wants to sanctify you completely. Body points to your physical life. Spirit is tied here to the mind, thought, and inner life. Soul points to the spiritual dimension of your being and your relation to God. But Paul is not slicing the person into three detachable entities.

You are one whole person. God wants your physical life, mental life, and spiritual life all brought into harmony under His grace.

So do not use this verse to prove an independently functioning soul inside the body. Use it to see God's desire to sanctify the whole person.

## Entry `ilk-024b`

- `question`: `How should the Sabbath be kept in places like Tonga where the international date line complicates the day sequence?`
- `scripture_basis`: `Genesis 2:1-3`
- `secondary_support`: `Selected Messages, book 3, pp. 317ff., "The Sabbath Made for a Round World"`
- `tags`: `Sabbath, Tonga, international-date-line, round-world, timekeeping, selected-messages`

### Answer

Keep the Sabbath faithfully within the real conditions of a round world, and do not turn a hard calendar case into a cause of division.

God established the seventh-day cycle at creation. But places affected by the international date line create real difficulties, just as unusual light conditions do in other parts of the world.

So honor the Sabbath conscientiously, follow the best settled counsel available, and remember that God takes into account the realities of sunrise, sunset, geography, and civil timekeeping.

Do not use a difficult case like Tonga to despise others or to create confusion. Keep the Sabbath in the best light available to you and do it faithfully before God.

## Entry `ilk-024c`

- `question`: `How would you answer the claim that the bronze serpent justifies making and bowing before religious images?`
- `scripture_basis`: `Numbers 21:8-9; John 3:14; Exodus 20:4-6; 2 Kings 18:4; 1 Corinthians 10:1-4`
- `tags`: `bronze-serpent, idols, images, Catholicism, worship, John-3, Exodus-20, 2-Kings-18`

### Answer

It does not justify image worship.

The bronze serpent was a temporary symbol commanded by God for a specific purpose. Jesus Himself explained that purpose in John 3:14: it pointed forward to Him being lifted up. The people were told to look and live, not to worship the serpent.

That symbol also proves the opposite of what image-veneration claims. Once the serpent was misused and became an object of devotion, it was destroyed. Second Kings 18 shows that it was not preserved as a permanent aid to worship.

So do not confuse symbolic typology with religious images used as objects of reverence. The serpent was a passing sign pointing to Christ. It never canceled the commandment against making images for worship, bowing before them, or giving them devotional honor.

If someone wants to use the bronze serpent to defend image veneration, the strongest answer is simple: the symbol was temporary, it pointed to Christ, it was never given to be worshiped, and when people started treating it wrongly, it was destroyed.
