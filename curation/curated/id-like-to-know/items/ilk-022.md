## Entry `ilk-022a`

- `question`: `What was Christ's work in the holy place, and after 1844 does He still intercede while ministering in the most holy place?`
- `scripture_basis`: `Hebrews 7:25; Hebrews 9:1-12; Acts 2:38; Leviticus 16; 1 John 1:9`
- `tags`: `sanctuary, holy-place, most-holy-place, 1844, intercession, atonement, Hebrews-9, Leviticus-16`

### Answer

Christ's work in the holy place was to apply to repentant sinners the benefits of what He accomplished on earth through His sinless life and sacrificial death.

The court comes first. There Christ lived the perfect life you need and died the death your sins deserved. Then in the holy place He ministers the value of that finished sacrifice to those who repent, confess, and come to God through Him. That is why Acts 2 still calls sinners to repent for the forgiveness of sins, and why Hebrews 7 says He always lives to make intercession.

So do not say that forgiveness was automatically handed to every person at the cross in the same way regardless of repentance. At the cross Christ made perfect provision for forgiveness. In His priestly ministry He applies that provision to believers.

After 1844 He did not stop interceding. In the most holy place He took on an additional phase of ministry, the work prefigured by the Day of Atonement. Leviticus 16 shows that even on that day the high priest still made atonement for himself, his house, and the people. The judgment phase did not cancel intercession.

So Christ does not need to move back and forth in order to keep helping sinners. He still intercedes while carrying forward the final phase of His heavenly ministry.

## Entry `ilk-022b`

- `question`: `Is it okay to ride a bicycle on the Sabbath?`
- `scripture_basis`: `Isaiah 58:13; Psalm 92; Matthew 12:1-8`
- `tags`: `Sabbath, bicycle, recreation, principles, Isaiah-58, Psalm-92, Matthew-12`

### Answer

Do not answer that question with a flat list of do's and don'ts. Answer it by Sabbath principle.

Isaiah 58 says you are not to do your own pleasure on God's holy day. Psalm 92 shows the Sabbath as a day of praise, gratitude, and delight in God. So the real question is not whether a bicycle is mechanically forbidden. The real question is what you are doing with it and why.

If the bicycle is simply a vehicle for God's work or for reaching another place in a way that serves the purpose of the Sabbath, that is one thing. Riding from village to village to give Bible studies is not the same as racing, showing off, mountain biking for thrills, or turning the day into a recreation day.

The same principle applies more broadly. Cooling off in water may be one thing if it is done quietly and reverently; splashing around for play is another. Heating food or getting medicine for someone in need may be appropriate; using the day as if it were an ordinary pleasure day is not.

So keep the Sabbath by principle, not by pharisaical hair-splitting. Do not make it miserable and legalistic. But do not baptize your own pleasure and call it Sabbath keeping either.
