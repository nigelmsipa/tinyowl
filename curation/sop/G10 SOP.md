### The Re-use of Jacob's Name (ilk-044)

While the sources detail that Jacob's name was changed to Israel ("a prince of God") as a commemoration of his victory of faith and the pardon of his sins at Peniel (*Thoughts from the Mount of Blessing*, "The Lord's Prayer"; *Faith and Works*, "Quality of Our Faith"), they do not explicitly explain why God continued to call him by his old name, Jacob, in subsequent interactions, such as in visions directing him to Egypt (*The Spirit of Prophecy, Vol. 1*, Chapter 16; *From Eternity Past*, Chapter 20).

### Does God Kill? Reconciling Justice and Love (ilk-059, ilk-084)

God does execute retributive judgment, and the sources affirm that "He will by no means clear the guilty" (*From Eternity Past*, Chapter 44; *The Spirit of Prophecy, Vol. 1*, Chapter 34). However, God's act of punishment is described as His "strange act" (*The Spirit of Prophecy, Vol. 1*, Chapter 34; *SDA Bible Commentary, Vol. 4*, Chapter 28). He takes no pleasure in the death of the wicked, but He is forced to execute judgment to preserve the inhabitants of the earth from utter depravity and ruin (*From Eternity Past*, Chapter 44; *The Spirit of Prophecy, Vol. 1*, Chapter 34). The destruction of the wicked is ultimately an act of mercy to the universe, preventing sin from being perpetuated eternally (*The Truth About Angels*, Chapter 24; *Last-Day Events*, Chapter 20). Furthermore, the sources clarify that death is the inevitable result of a sinner's own choice; by choosing sin, one cuts oneself off from God, the fountain of life, and the very presence of a holy God becomes to them a consuming fire (*From Heaven With Love*, Chapter 87; *The Truth About Angels*, Chapter 24).

Regarding the moral justification for Old Testament killing orders (like the Canaanites or the Flood), God has unlimited control over all the works of His hands, and "there is no injustice in His character" (*Selected Messages, Book 2*, Chapter 39; *SDA Bible Commentary, Vol. 1*, EGW Notes on Joshua). God is incredibly long-suffering. He spared the Amorites for four hundred years to give them unmistakable evidence of the true God, waiting until "the iniquity of the Amorites is not yet full" (*SDA Bible Commentary, Vol. 1*, EGW Notes on Genesis 15; *Sketches from the Life of Paul*, Chapter 23; *The Spirit of Prophecy, Vol. 4*, Chapter 2). When their guilt reached its limit and they clung to brutal idolatry—such as burning their children in the fire (*Spiritual Gifts, Vol. 3*, Chapter 23)—they were destroyed. Similarly, in the days of Noah, men's long lives only led them to multiply their violence and corruption; God destroyed them to cleanse the earth of moral pollution (*Testimony Studies on Diet and Foods*, "General Principles"; *The Story of Redemption*, Chapter 7). Unconditional pardon for such high-handed rebellion would be weakness, not benevolence, and would fill the unfallen universe with consternation (*The Spirit of Prophecy, Vol. 4*, Chapter 2; *The Great Controversy 1888*, Chapter 1).

### The Trinity and the Godhead (ilk-106)

Yes, the sources explicitly affirm Trinitarian theology. There are "three living persons of the heavenly trio" (*SDA Bible Commentary, Vol. 7A*, Section 3; *Evangelism*, Section 18). These "eternal heavenly dignitaries" are identified as God the Father, Christ the Son, and the Holy Spirit (*SDA Bible Commentary, Vol. 7A*, Section 3; *Manuscript 145, 1901*). Christ is "God essentially, and in the highest sense," existing with the Father from all eternity (*The Desire of Ages*, Chapter 1; *Selected Messages, Book 1*, Chapter 34). At baptism, believers enter a covenant in the "threefold name of the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit," and these three great powers pledge to cooperate with the Christian in overcoming darkness (*SDA Bible Commentary, Vol. 7A*, Section 3; *Testimonies for the Church, Vol. 6*, Chapter 10).

### God's Name: Jehovah and I AM (ilk-107)

The sources refer to the Supreme Being as "Jehovah, the eternal, self-existent, uncreated One" (*Patriarchs and Prophets*, Chapter 22; *The Spirit of Prophecy, Vol. 1*, Chapter 12). Christ also announced Himself to Moses and the Jews under the title "I AM THAT I AM" (*The Desire of Ages*, Chapter 1; *The Spirit of Prophecy, Vol. 2*, Chapter 1; *Sanctuary, 1844, and Investigative Judgment*, "The God of the Burning Bush"). The title "I AM" signifies an "eternal presence," demonstrating that the past, present, and future are alike to God (*The Desire of Ages*, Chapter 1).

### Seeing God in the Old Testament (ilk-182)

The Bible states that no mortal can see the unveiled glory of God and live (*The Spirit of Prophecy, Vol. 1*, Chapter 12; *Patriarchs and Prophets*, Chapter 27). To communicate with fallen humanity without consuming them, "the all-merciful God shrouded His glory in a most humble type" (*The Desire of Ages*, Chapter 1; *The Spirit of Prophecy, Vol. 1*, Chapter 12). He veiled His majesty in the burning bush and in the pillar of cloud by day and fire by night (*The Desire of Ages*, Chapter 1; *The Spirit of Prophecy, Vol. 1*, Chapter 15). Furthermore, it was Christ who mediated this presence. Christ was the "Angel of God's presence" who spoke to Moses and guided Israel in the wilderness, veiling the dazzling splendor of His divinity so that humanity could become acquainted with their Creator (*Patriarchs and Prophets*, Appendix, Note 3; *Historical Sketches of Foreign Missions*, "Labors in Europe"; *The Spirit of Prophecy, Vol. 1*, Chapter 12). Even when Moses pleaded to see God's glory, God mercifully covered him in the cleft of a rock and only allowed him to see His subdued glory (*Patriarchs and Prophets*, Chapter 27; *The Spirit of Prophecy, Vol. 1*, Chapter 18).

### The "First Light" of Genesis 1:3 (ilk-208)

The sources describe that at the creative word, "light had shone out of darkness" and "the Spirit of God moved upon the face of the waters" to bring forth light (*Selected Messages, Book 3*, Chapter 4; *The Spirit of Prophecy, Vol. 1*, Chapter 1). Christ Himself is described as the one who caused this light to shine (*The Spirit of Prophecy, Vol. 1*, Chapter 1).

### God and Ethnic Favoritism (ilk-213)

God is "no respecter of persons," but in every generation those who fear Him and work righteousness are accepted (*Acts of the Apostles*, Chapter 14; *Historical Sketches of Foreign Missions*, "Address at Orebro"). He has a "tender pity" for all and "makes no difference between white and black" (*Sanctuary, 1844, and Investigative Judgment*, "Ethnic Equality"). While God anciently chose the Israelites as a special people, it was not for selfish exclusivity; He chose them to be depositaries of His law and light-bearers so that He might be revealed to all nations (*Acts of the Apostles*, Chapter 2; *The Desire of Ages*, Chapter 2). The covenant with Abraham embraced all the nations of the earth (*From Eternity Past*, Chapter 7). Because the Jewish nation failed this purpose, God's true Israel is now made up of all who yield themselves to Him, regardless of whether they are Jew or Gentile (*From Trials to Triumph*, Chapter 18; *From Splendor to Shadow*, Chapter 30; *Acts of the Apostles*, Chapter 35). There is no arbitrary election where anyone is excluded; every soul is elected who chooses to obey God (*The Spirit of Prophecy, Vol. 2*, Chapter 1; *SDA Bible Commentary, Vol. 6*, EGW Notes on Ephesians 1).

### Who Should We Pray To? (ilk-214)

The sources instruct believers to address the Father through the mediation of the Son. Christ taught man to address the Supreme Ruler by the endearing name, "Our Father" (*Thoughts from the Mount of Blessing*, "The Lord's Prayer"; *Testimonies for the Church, Vol. 6*, Chapter 45). However, because sin separated man from God, we cannot approach Him on our own; we must "present your case to the Father through Jesus Christ" (*Prayer*, Chapter 2). Christ stands before the eternal throne as our Advocate and High Priest, presenting our prayers as spiritual sacrifices (*Sanctuary, 1844, and Investigative Judgment*, "Mediation and Prayer"; *Counsels for the Church*, Chapter 64). Therefore, we pray to the Father in the name and merits of the Son (*Acts of the Apostles*, Chapter 3; *Steps to Christ*, Chapter 11).

### God Portrayed as Male (ilk-219)

Throughout the sources, God is referred to with male pronouns (He, Him) and is characterized by His "paternal love" and "tender Fatherhood" (*Thoughts from the Mount of Blessing*, "The Lord's Prayer"; *The Ministry of Healing*, Chapter 40; *Testimonies for the Church, Vol. 8*, Chapter 43). He is likened to a father who pities his children (*Sanctuary, 1844, and Investigative Judgment*, "Mediation and Prayer"; *Steps to Christ*, Chapter 1).
