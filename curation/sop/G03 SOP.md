**Body, Soul, Spirit, and Consciousness in Death (ilk-024, ilk-201)**
The biblical doctrine of sanctification encompasses the entire being—spirit, soul, and body—as one mortal whole, not as separate immortal entities (*The Sanctified Life*, "True and False Theories Contrasted"). The idea of a naturally immortal soul is identified as Satan's original deception in Eden when he told Eve, "Ye shall not surely die" (*The Great Controversy*, "The First Great Deception"). The Bible clearly states that "the soul that sinneth, it shall die" (Ezekiel 18:20), proving that the soul is subject to death and is not inherently immortal (*The Great Controversy*, "The First Great Deception"; *Life Sketches of Ellen G. White*, "The Immortality Question").

Regarding the human spirit after death, the Scriptures declare that man is not conscious: "His breath goeth forth, he returneth to his earth; in that very day his thoughts perish" (Psalm 146:4) (*The Great Controversy*, "The First Great Deception"). The dead know absolutely nothing, and their love, hatred, and envy perish (*The Great Controversy*, "The First Great Deception"). At death, the "spirit, the character of man, is returned to God, there to be preserved" until the resurrection, but it does not maintain a separate, conscious existence (*SDA Bible Commentary, vol. 6*, Comments on 1 Corinthians 15). To the believer, death is merely a sleep—a moment of silence and darkness until the Life-giver returns (*The Desire of Ages*, "Lazarus, Come Forth"; *From Heaven With Love*, "Jesus and the Woman With Five Husbands").

**Jesus' Teachings and His Death (ilk-047, ilk-180)**
Jesus did not contradict the unconscious state of the dead (Ecclesiastes 9:5). In John 11, when speaking of Lazarus, Christ explicitly represented death as a sleep (*The Desire of Ages*, "Lazarus, Come Forth"). He plainly stated, "Lazarus is dead," and He directed Martha's hope not to an immediate heavenly reward, but to the future resurrection at the last day (*The Desire of Ages*, "Lazarus, Come Forth").

Likewise, when Jesus died on the cross and said, "Father, into thy hands I commend my spirit" (Luke 23:46), His soul did not instantly go to heaven (*SDA Bible Commentary, vol. 5*, Comments on Luke 23). The sources affirm: "The spirit of Jesus slept in the tomb with His body, and did not wing its way to heaven, there to maintain a separate existence... All that comprised the life and intelligence of Jesus remained with His body in the sepulcher" (*SDA Bible Commentary, vol. 5*, Comments on John 10; *The Spirit of Prophecy, vol. 3*, "The Resurrection of Christ"). After His resurrection, Jesus Himself declared, "I am not yet ascended to my Father," proving He rested unconsciously in the tomb over the Sabbath (*SDA Bible Commentary, vol. 5*, Comments on John 10).

**Cremation, Organ Donation, and the Resurrection Body (ilk-102, ilk-139)**
*(Please note: The sources do not explicitly use the modern terms "cremation" or "organ donation," but they provide clear theological principles governing the physical body and the resurrection.)*

The method of body disposal does not affect the resurrection. The sources explicitly state that "there is no law of God in nature which shows that God gives back the same identical particles of matter which composed the body before death" (*SDA Bible Commentary, vol. 6*, Comments on 1 Corinthians 15). Using the Apostle Paul's illustration of a planted seed that decays to bring forth a new kernel, the texts explain that "the natural substance in the grain that decays is never raised as before, but God giveth it a body as it hath pleased Him" (*SDA Bible Commentary, vol. 6*, Comments on 1 Corinthians 15). Therefore, physical destruction or decay poses no barrier to God, who preserves the individual's character and will provide a new, perfect spiritual body (*SDA Bible Commentary, vol. 6*, Comments on 1 Corinthians 15; *The Great Controversy*, "God's People Delivered").

By extension, while organ donation is not named, the principles state that our bodies belong to God and we are to use them to bless humanity (*Medical Ministry*, "The Worker's Health"; *Counsels on Health*, "God's People to Stand in Purity"). The lack of original physical matter at the resurrection means that missing organs or ashes will not hinder God's re-creative power.

**Satan's Immortality and the Timing of Punishment (ilk-113, ilk-175)**
Satan does not possess inherent immortality. Because of his rebellion, God has declared to him: "I will bring thee to ashes upon the earth... and never shalt thou be any more" (Ezekiel 28:18, 19) (*The Great Controversy*, "The First Great Deception"). At the end of the one thousand years, Satan and his followers will be entirely consumed in the lake of fire—Satan the root, and his followers the branches (*A Word to the Little Flock*, "To Bro. Eli Curtis"; *Early Writings*, "The Second Death").

Consequently, the lost are not being actively punished right now. "Nowhere in the Sacred Scriptures is found the statement that the righteous go to their reward or the wicked to their punishment at death" (*The Great Controversy*, "The First Great Deception"). The wicked sleep in their graves until the second resurrection at the end of the millennium, at which point they will be judged and suffer the "second death"—an eternal destruction, not an eternal life of ongoing misery (*The Great Controversy*, "The Controversy Ended"; *Early Writings*, "The Second Death"). The doctrine of a currently burning hell is described as a pagan heresy that maligns God's character of love (*The Great Controversy*, "The First Great Deception").

**Spiritualism and Old Testament Appearances (ilk-161, ilk-162)**
Christianity and spiritualism cannot coexist. The Bible strictly forbids communication with familiar spirits, declaring it an abomination punishable by death in the Old Testament (*Patriarchs and Prophets*, "Death of Saul"; *The Great Controversy*, "Can Our Dead Speak to Us?"). Because the dead are completely unconscious, the entities that appear in séances are not deceased loved ones, but rather "the spirits of devils"—fallen angels who impersonate the dead to deceive humanity (*The Story of Redemption*, "The First Great Deception"; *The Great Controversy*, "Can Our Dead Speak to Us?").

This explains Old Testament events such as the Witch of Endor. When Saul sought the deceased prophet Samuel, "Satan was not allowed to disturb the rest of Samuel in the grave... God does not give Satan power to resurrect the dead. But Satan's angels assume the form of dead friends" (*Patriarchs and Prophets*, "Death of Saul"). The apparition was a demonic counterfeit (*Patriarchs and Prophets*, "Death of Saul").

Furthermore, when Jesus referenced God's Old Testament title as the "God of Abraham, and the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob" to prove the resurrection to the Sadducees, He was not implying these patriarchs were currently conscious in heaven. Instead, "God counts the things that are not as though they were. He sees the end from the beginning... The precious dead... will hear the voice of the Son of God, and will come forth from the grave to immortal life... This condition, which is anticipated in His purpose, He beholds as if it were already existing. The dead live unto Him" (*The Desire of Ages*, "Controversy").
