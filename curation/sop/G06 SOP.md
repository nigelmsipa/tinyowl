**The 2300-Day Prophecy, William Miller, and the 1844 Disappointment (ilk-001, ilk-054, ilk-093, ilk-168, ilk-223)**
To understand the 1844 investigative judgment from scripture alone, one must interpret the symbolic language of Bible prophecy using the Bible itself (*The Great Controversy*, "An American Reformer"). In prophetic symbolism, a "day" equals a literal solar "year" (Ezekiel 4:6; Numbers 14:34) (*The Great Controversy*, "An American Reformer"). Furthermore, a "beast" represents a kingdom, "waters" represent multitudes of people, and a "woman" represents a church (*The Great Controversy*, "An American Reformer").

Applying the year-day principle to the prophecy of Daniel 8:14—"Unto two thousand and three hundred days; then shall the sanctuary be cleansed"—yields a period of 2300 literal years (*The Great Controversy*, "An American Reformer"). The starting point for this prophecy is found in Daniel 9, where 70 weeks (490 years) are "cut off" from the 2300 days, beginning with the decree to restore and rebuild Jerusalem in 457 B.C. (*The Great Controversy*, "An American Reformer"). Calculating 2300 years from the autumn of 457 B.C. extends exactly to the autumn of A.D. 1844 (*The Great Controversy*, "An American Reformer").

William Miller did not preach a lie; his calculation of the prophetic timeline was completely accurate (*The Great Controversy*, "An American Reformer"). His error was regarding the event that was to take place. He accepted the popular view that the "sanctuary" was the earth, and concluded that the earth would be cleansed by fire at Christ's second coming in 1844 (*The Great Controversy*, "What is the Sanctuary?"). However, the sanctuary to be cleansed was actually the heavenly sanctuary (*The Great Controversy*, "What is the Sanctuary?"). The "cleansing" refers to the antitypical Day of Atonement—the final phase of Christ's high priestly ministry where He enters the Most Holy Place to cleanse the heavenly records of sin, commencing the investigative judgment (*The Great Controversy*, "What is the Sanctuary?"). God permitted the Great Disappointment to test the hearts of His people, much like the disciples were bitterly disappointed when Christ was crucified instead of taking an earthly throne (*The Great Controversy*, "Prophecies Fulfilled").

Following 1844, William Miller rejected further light regarding the third angel's message and the Sabbath (*Early Writings*, "William Miller"). However, the sources note that he was worn, ill, and heavily influenced by associates who opposed the truth (*Early Writings*, "William Miller"). God mercifully allowed him to fall asleep in death to hide him from those leading him astray; angels watch over his dust, and he will be saved and raised at the sound of the last trumpet (*Early Writings*, "William Miller").

**The Heavenly Sanctuary, the Temple, and the Ark (ilk-014, ilk-018, ilk-179)**
The physical temple in Jerusalem was destroyed by the Romans in A.D. 70 (*The Great Controversy*, "The Destruction of Jerusalem"). A rebuilt literal temple in the Middle East does not feature in end-time events (*The Great Controversy*, "The Destruction of Jerusalem"). The entire earthly sacrificial system lost its significance at the crucifixion when the inner veil of the temple was rent from top to bottom, signifying that the type had met the antitype (*The Desire of Ages*, "It Is Finished"). The focus of the universe is now on the heavenly temple where Christ ministers (*The Great Controversy*, "What is the Sanctuary?").

Just prior to the destruction of Solomon's temple, faithful men secretly hid the earthly Ark of the Covenant in a cave (*Prophets and Kings*, "Jeremiah"). It has never been disturbed since (*Prophets and Kings*, "Jeremiah"). However, the earthly ark was merely a copy. Revelation 11:19 states, "the temple of God was opened in heaven, and there was seen in his temple the ark of his testament" (*The Great Controversy*, "God's Law Immutable"). The true Ark of the Covenant is safely enshrined in the Most Holy Place of the heavenly sanctuary, containing the original Ten Commandments written by the finger of God (*The Great Controversy*, "God's Law Immutable"; *Early Writings*, "The Sanctuary").

The 24 elders mentioned in Revelation 4–5 are literal beings (*The Desire of Ages*, "To My Father, and Your Father"). They represent the unfallen worlds or those redeemed from among men—the "first fruits" who were raised from the grave when Christ arose and ascended with Him to serve in the heavenly courts (*The Desire of Ages*, "To My Father, and Your Father").

**The Investigative Judgment: Records, Assurance, and Relevance (ilk-043, ilk-115, ilk-159, ilk-174)**
The investigative judgment is of infinite importance to Christians today because we are living in the great antitypical Day of Atonement (*The Great Controversy*, "Facing Life's Record"). The cases of all who have ever professed faith in Christ are pending at the bar of God, and probation will soon close forever (*The Great Controversy*, "Facing Life's Record").

In the heavenly sanctuary, exact "books of record" are kept (*The Great Controversy*, "Facing Life's Record"). Opposite every name is recorded with terrible exactness every wrong word, selfish act, unfulfilled duty, and secret motive (*The Great Controversy*, "Facing Life's Record"). During the investigative judgment, these records are reviewed against the standard of God's law (*The Great Controversy*, "Facing Life's Record"). For those whose sins remain unrepented of, their names will be blotted out of the book of life (*The Great Controversy*, "Facing Life's Record"). But for those who have truly repented and claimed the blood of Christ, their sins are blotted out from the records, and they are accounted worthy of eternal life (*The Great Controversy*, "Facing Life's Record").

This judgment is entirely compatible with salvation and assurance in Christ (*Faith and Works*, "Christ Our Righteousness"). Christ serves not only as our Judge but as our Advocate (1 John 2:1) (*The Great Controversy*, "Facing Life's Record"). He does not excuse our sins, but He pleads His wounded hands and His perfect, spotless righteousness on behalf of the penitent believer (*The Great Controversy*, "Facing Life's Record"; *Faith and Works*, "Christ Our Righteousness"). Our assurance is not found in human perfection, but in Christ's imparted and imputed righteousness (*Faith and Works*, "Christ Our Righteousness").

Critics like Desmond Ford have historically objected to the investigative judgment, arguing that Christ entered the Most Holy Place immediately at His ascension and that Daniel 8:14 merely refers to the desecration of the earthly temple by Antiochus Epiphanes (*The Great Controversy*, "What is the Sanctuary?"). The biblical response maintains that the Old Testament sanctuary explicitly had a two-phase ministry (daily in the Holy Place, yearly in the Most Holy), which Hebrews 8 and 9 confirm is the pattern for the heavenly reality (*The Great Controversy*, "What is the Sanctuary?"). Furthermore, Daniel 8 and 9 link the 2300 days to the 70 weeks, establishing an eschatological prophetic timeline reaching to 1844 and the hour of God's judgment, far beyond Antiochus (*The Great Controversy*, "What is the Sanctuary?").

**Theological Developments in 1888 (ilk-202)**
In 1888, at the Minneapolis General Conference, a profound theological shift occurred. God sent a precious message through Elders E.J. Waggoner and A.T. Jones regarding Righteousness by Faith (*Testimonies to Ministers and Gospel Workers*, "Righteousness by Faith"). This mattered significantly because the church had been in danger of preaching the law while being "almost as destitute of the knowledge of Jesus Christ and His relation to the law as was the offering of Cain" (*Testimonies to Ministers and Gospel Workers*, "Righteousness by Faith"). The 1888 message sought to lift up Jesus, emphasizing His matchless love and His imputed righteousness as a free gift, designed to bring the church back to its "first love" and prepare it to give the final Loud Cry to the world (*Testimonies to Ministers and Gospel Workers*, "Righteousness by Faith").
