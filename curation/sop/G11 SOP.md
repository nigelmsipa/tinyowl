**The Prophetic Gift After the Apostolic Age (ilk-012, ilk-205)**
The prophetic gift was never designed to be limited to the primitive church or the apostolic age. The Great Commission given by Christ, which promised that miraculous signs would follow believers, extends unto the end of the world (*The Great Controversy*, "Preface"). The Apostle Paul taught that spiritual gifts were "set in the church" (1 Corinthians 12:28) to perfect the saints and bring them to a unity of the faith, and these gifts will not cease until the perfect state of the immortal world is reached (*Early Writings*, "Spiritual Gifts"). The rarity of these gifts over the centuries was due to the apostasy, errors, and unbelief of the church, not because God withdrew them (*The Spirit of Prophecy, vol. 1*, "The Spirit of Prophecy").

Joel 2:28 promises that in the "last days" God will pour out His Spirit upon all flesh, causing sons and daughters to prophesy, young men to see visions, and old men to dream dreams. While this saw a partial fulfillment at Pentecost, it reaches its full accomplishment in the "latter rain" which will attend the closing work of the gospel just prior to the great and terrible day of the Lord (*Christian Experience and Teachings of Ellen G. White*, "The Prophetic Gift"). Furthermore, Revelation 12:17 declares that the dragon will make war with the remnant (the last generation of the church), who "keep the commandments of God, and have the testimony of Jesus Christ" (*The Great Controversy 1888*, "The Two Witnesses"). Revelation 19:10 explicitly defines this "testimony of Jesus" as "the spirit of prophecy" (*Early Writings*, "Spiritual Gifts"). Therefore, the remnant church will be distinguished by the revival of the prophetic gift.

**Relationship Between Ellen White and the Bible (ilk-068)**
Ellen White never claimed authority above the Scriptures; instead, she consistently pointed to the Bible as the "supreme authority," the "unerring standard," and the "rule of your faith and practice" by which all teachings, experiences, and spiritual manifestations must be tested (*Selected Messages Book 3*, "The Primacy of the Word"). She defined her writings as "a lesser light to lead men and women to the greater light" (*Colporteur Ministry*, "Books That Give the Message"). She explicitly stated that her testimonies were not given to take the place of the Bible or to provide new doctrines. Rather, because people had neglected the Word of God and failed to practice its plain teachings, God provided the testimonies to bring them back to the Bible, to simplify its great truths, and to impress its principles vividly upon the heart (*Selected Messages Book 1*, "The Inspiration of the Prophetic Writers"). She even instructed ministers not to quote her writings as the primary authority to sustain their doctrinal positions, but to bring their clear proofs directly from the Word of God (*Selected Messages Book 3*, "The Primacy of the Word").

**The Nature of Ellen White's Inspiration (ilk-066)**
Not every word spoken or letter written by Ellen White was equally inspired. When questioned if every letter she wrote under all circumstances was "as inspired as the Ten Commandments," she firmly denied it (*Selected Messages Book 1*, "Ellen G. White and Her Writings"). She clarified that the Ten Commandments were of direct divine composition, whereas the Bible—and her own writings—represent a union of the divine and the human. God inspired the *thoughts* and visions, but the writers expressed those truths using their own imperfect human language and mental development (*Selected Messages Book 1*, "Ellen G. White and Her Writings").

However, regarding her published books, articles, and testimonies of reproof, she firmly maintained that they were not merely her human opinions. She stated: "I do not write one article in the paper expressing merely my own ideas. They are what God has opened before me in vision" (*Selected Messages Book 1*, "Ellen G. White and Her Writings"). She strongly warned against those who attempted to dissect her testimonies to define what was from God and what was her own human judgment, declaring that those who do this are helping the devil in his work of deception (*Selected Messages Book 3*, "Defining Sister White's Judgment and the Word of the Lord").

**Literary Assistants and the Use of Historians (ilk-003)**
In the preparation of her books, Ellen White used literary assistants (like Marian Davis) to gather her previously written materials, correct grammar, and compile chapters (*Selected Messages Book 3*, "Literary Assistants in Work of Ellen G. White"). However, these copy editors did not change her language, add their own thoughts, or alter the meaning of the messages; her words stood as she wrote them (*Selected Messages Book 3*, Appendix C). When describing historical events, such as the Reformation, the great outlines of the controversy were shown to her in vision. As she wrote out these scenes, she sometimes found that conscientious historians had provided perfect descriptions of the events and facts that God had already revealed to her. She utilized their language because it was a convenient and forceful way to present the truth, but she never intended for her writings to be used as an infallible authority on historical dates or minor chronological details (*Selected Messages Book 3*, Appendix B).

**Christmas Trees (ilk-067)**
Ellen White did not condemn Christmas trees. She wrote that there is "no particular sin in selecting a fragrant evergreen and placing it in our churches." The sin lies in the motive for doing so and the use made of the gifts (*The Adventist Home*, "Christmas"). Instead of using the tree for mere amusement or to mimic the extravagant display of the world, she advised that its boughs "be laden with the golden and silver fruit of your beneficence" and presented to God as an offering to sustain His work and help the needy (*The Adventist Home*, "Christmas").

**Life Insurance (ilk-126)**
Ellen White counseled Sabbathkeeping Adventists not to engage in commercial life insurance. She described it as a "worldly policy" and a "commerce with the world which God does not approve" because it weakens faith, lessens spirituality, and demonstrates a distrust of God's providence (*Testimonies for the Church, vol. 1*, "Life Insurance"). Furthermore, she pointed out that it diverts means belonging to God into the enemy's ranks instead of investing them in His cause (*Testimonies for the Church, vol. 1*, "Life Insurance"). Instead, she pointed believers to the "eternal life insurance policy" provided by Christ's sacrifice, which guarantees immortality and a life measuring with the life of God (*SDA Bible Commentary, vol. 7*, "Chapter 1").

**Jesus Between the Resurrection and Ascension (ilk-212)**
During the forty days after His resurrection and before His ascension, Jesus remained with His disciples, showing Himself alive by "many infallible proofs" and speaking to them of the things "pertaining to the kingdom of God" (*The Youth's Instructor*, "By Many Infallible Proofs"). He used this time to open their understanding to the Old Testament Scriptures, explaining in "all the prophets" the things concerning Himself (*The Desire of Ages*, "To My Father, and Your Father"). He demonstrated that He was the true sin-bearer and, having conquered death, led forth a multitude of captives from the grave as "jewels gathered from the earth" who appeared unto many in Jerusalem before ascending to heaven with Him (*The Youth's Instructor*, "By Many Infallible Proofs").
