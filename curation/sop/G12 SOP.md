**Use of Tithe for Personal Retirement (ilk-030)**
Tithe money cannot be used for personal retirement savings. The tithe is sacred, reserved by God for Himself, and is to be brought into His treasury exclusively to sustain gospel laborers and ministers in their work (*Testimonies for the Church, vol. 9*, "Faithful Stewardship"). Believers are strictly warned that they are not to divert it to any other purpose, nor are they at liberty to retain their tithe to use according to their own judgment or for themselves in an emergency (*Testimonies to Ministers and Gospel Workers*, "Means and Methods").

**Did Jesus Pay Tithe? (ilk-051)**
Yes, Christ recognized the payment of tithes as a duty, and the sources affirm that it was Christ Himself who gave all the directions of the law in regard to tithes and offerings (*The Desire of Ages*, "Tradition"). While the texts do not detail a specific instance of Jesus paying a tithe on personal income, they emphasize that He consistently upheld the tithing system. When rebuking the Pharisees for their burdensome rules, He confirmed the obligation to tithe, saying, "these ought ye to have done" (*The Desire of Ages*, "Woe Unto You, Pharisees!"). Furthermore, He strictly obeyed God's requirements and the laws of the land, miraculously providing a coin to pay the annual temple tribute for Himself and Peter so as not to give offense (*The Desire of Ages*, "The Temple Tribute").

**Tithing on Borrowed Money (ilk-056)**
God specifically asks for one-tenth of all our increase (*Testimonies for the Church, vol. 3*, "Tithes and Offerings"). Since borrowed money is a debt and not an actual increase or profit, the principle of tithing the increase would not apply to the principal of a loan. However, the sources offer stern warnings regarding debt and stewardship. Being in debt does not excuse a person from paying tithes on their actual increase. A person who finds himself in debt should not take the Lord's portion to cancel his debts to his fellow men, as this makes him a double debtor who robs God (*Testimonies for the Church, vol. 6*, "Giving to God His Own").

**Biblical Financial Stewardship Beyond the Tithe (ilk-074)**
True financial stewardship extends far beyond the tenth, recognizing that God is the Creator and proprietor of the earth, and everything we possess is His (*Patriarchs and Prophets*, "Tithes and Offerings"). Beyond the tithe, stewardship includes bringing freewill offerings, thank offerings, and trespass offerings in proportion to how God has blessed us (*Testimonies for the Church, vol. 5*, "Will a Man Rob God?"). Historically, God also required a second tithe used for charity, hospitality, and entertaining the Levites and the poor (*Patriarchs and Prophets*, "God's Care for the Poor"). Furthermore, Christian stewardship requires caring for the helpless and practicing strict economy and frugality—shunning debt, extravagance, and needless luxuries so that more means can be used to advance God's cause (*The Adventist Home*, "Economy to be Practiced").

**Stealing to Give to God (ilk-164)**
It is never acceptable to steal or use dishonest means to give money to God. The Lord declares, "I hate robbery for burnt offering" (*Testimonies for the Church, vol. 4*, "Dishonesty in the Church"). God will not accept money obtained by overreaching in trade, fraud, or the liquor traffic; such money is stained with the blood of souls and has the curse of God upon it (*The Ministry of Healing*, "Liquor Traffic and Prohibition"). A man may excuse his avarice by claiming he is working for the cause of God, but he obtains no reward, for God does not want money obtained by any semblance of dishonesty (*Testimonies for the Church, vol. 4*, "Uprightness in Deal").

**Withholding Tithe from a Corrupt Church or Leadership (ilk-184, ilk-225)**
Withholding tithe because of a lack of confidence in church leadership or perceived corruption is not justified. The texts explicitly address this: "Some have been dissatisfied, and have said, 'I will not longer pay my tithe; for I have no confidence in the way things are managed at the heart of the work.' But will you rob God because you think the management of the work is not right?" (*Testimonies to Ministers and Gospel Workers*, "Means and Methods"). If there is mismanagement, believers are instructed to make their complaints plainly and openly to the proper authorities, but they must not withdraw from God's work or prove unfaithful (*Testimonies for the Church, vol. 9*, "Faithful Stewardship"). The tithes are the property of God, not of any man, and even if unworthy ministers receive some of the funds, no one should dare to withhold from the treasury and brave the curse of God (*Pastoral Ministry*, "Finance"). The recording angel notes the sincere motive of the giver, so the faithful donor will not lose their reward even if the means are misapplied by leadership (*Counsels on Sabbath School Work*, "Gathering Weekly Mission Offerings").

**The Biblical Basis for Tithing (ilk-185)**
The tithing system did not originate with Moses but reaches back to the earliest times (*Patriarchs and Prophets*, "Tithes and Offerings"). Abraham paid tithes to Melchizedek, and Jacob vowed a tenth to God at Bethel (*Testimonies for the Church, vol. 3*, "Tithes and Offerings"). It was reaffirmed as a divine statute in the Mosaic law, declaring that the tithe is holy unto the Lord (*Patriarchs and Prophets*, "Tithes and Offerings"). In the prophetic books, Malachi explicitly commands bringing all the tithes into the storehouse, warning of a curse for robbery and promising a blessing for obedience (*Testimonies for the Church, vol. 6*, "Giving to God His Own"). In the New Testament, Christ upheld the tithe when rebuking the Pharisees, and Paul reaffirmed the principle by comparing the support of gospel ministers to the tithing system of the Levitical priesthood (*The Desire of Ages*, "Woe Unto You, Pharisees!"; *The Acts of the Apostles*, "A Liberal Church").

**Why We Tithe: Law, Gratitude, or Covenant? (ilk-218)**
We tithe for all of these reasons combined. First, it is an absolute divine law and a command; God simply states, "This is Mine," and withholding it is classified as embezzlement and robbery (*Counsels for the Church*, "Counsels on Stewardship"). Second, it is an act of gratitude, an acknowledgment that God is the Creator, the proprietor of the earth, and the giver of health, sunshine, and rain (*Patriarchs and Prophets*, "Tithes and Offerings"). Finally, it is covenant participation. God instituted systematic benevolence for man's own benefit, to tear away his natural selfishness and counteract the dangerous sin of covetousness (*Testimonies for the Church, vol. 3*, "Tithes and Offerings"). Continual giving assimilates the believer into the image of Christ, drawing them into a collaborative covenant where they become "co-workers with Him" in the salvation of the world (*Testimonies for the Church, vol. 3*, "Tithes and Offerings").
