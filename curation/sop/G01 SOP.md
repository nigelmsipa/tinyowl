Here is a comprehensive response to your queries regarding the biblical identity and theological defense of the Sabbath, based on the provided sources:

**ilk-025 — The March Around Jericho on the Sabbath**
The sources do not classify the march around Jericho as a Sabbath violation, but rather as an act of absolute obedience to a direct command from Christ, the "Captain of the Lord's host" (*Patriarchs and Prophets*, Chapter 45: The Fall of Jericho). The event was designed by God to demonstrate that the victory was entirely His and not attained by human prowess or conventional warfare (*Patriarchs and Prophets*, Chapter 45: The Fall of Jericho). The Israelites were instructed to silently march and believe, while the armies of heaven actually threw down the walls (*Testimonies for the Church, vol. 4*, Chapter 16: The Test of Faith). This episode reveals that when God directly commands a specific action—even on the Sabbath—it is an exercise of His sovereign authority, and His people's role is to trust and obey (*Patriarchs and Prophets*, Chapter 45: The Fall of Jericho).

**ilk-034, ilk-177, & ilk-200 — The Ceremonial Law vs. The Moral Law**
The New Testament clearly distinguishes between the immutable moral law (the Ten Commandments) and the temporary ceremonial law. Ephesians 2:14–16 and Colossians 2:14–17 refer to the ceremonial system—the types, shadows, and sacrifices that pointed forward to the death of Christ (*Patriarchs and Prophets*, Chapter 32: The Law and the Covenants). When Christ died on the cross, "type met antitype," and the ceremonial law ("the law of commandments contained in ordinances") was abolished and "nailed to His cross" (*The Great Controversy*, Chapter 18: An American Reformer). However, the abolition of the ceremonial law provides no grounds for abandoning the moral law or the Sabbath. Christ's death proved the immutability of the Ten Commandments; if the moral law could have been changed or set aside to save humanity, Jesus would not have had to die (*The Great Controversy*, Chapter 25: God's Law Immutable). Thus, Paul was defending believers against legalistic judgment regarding the Jewish ceremonial traditions, not abolishing the seventh-day Sabbath (*Patriarchs and Prophets*, Chapter 32: The Law and the Covenants).

**ilk-042 & ilk-149 — The Boundaries of the Sabbath**
The precise biblical basis for the Sabbath boundary is from sundown to sundown. The sources explicitly state: "From even unto even, shall ye celebrate your Sabbath" (*Testimonies for the Church, vol. 6*, Chapter 44: The Observance of the Sabbath). This is not merely a Jewish tradition but a creation pattern, originating in the first week of time where "the evening and the morning were the first day" (*Patriarchs and Prophets*, Chapter 9: The Literal Week). To properly keep the day, all secular work and preparation must be laid aside "before the setting of the sun" on Friday, and the close of the Sabbath is similarly marked "as the sun goes down" (*Testimonies for the Church, vol. 6*, Chapter 44: The Observance of the Sabbath).

**ilk-085 — The Change of Priesthood**
While the earthly Levitical priesthood and its sacrificial oblations ceased at the cross (*The Great Controversy*, Chapter 24: In the Holy of Holies), this did not nullify the Sabbath. Christ transitioned to become the Minister of the new covenant in the heavenly sanctuary (*The Great Controversy*, Chapter 23: What is the Sanctuary?). In this heavenly temple, the original Ten Commandments are enshrined in the ark of the covenant, with the fourth commandment shining brightly (*Early Writings*, Chapter: The Sanctuary). The change in priesthood pertained only to the mediation of salvation, not the standard of righteousness.

**ilk-116 — A Universal Commandment**
The Sabbath is a universal commandment for all humanity. "The Sabbath was not for Israel merely, but for the world" (*The Desire of Ages*, Chapter 29: The Sabbath). It was instituted in Eden before the Fall, making it a foundational memorial of creation that was committed to Adam as the "father and representative of the whole human family" (*Patriarchs and Prophets*, Chapter 2: The Creation). The Israelites were required to observe it and were even tested on it through the miracle of the manna before they ever reached Mount Sinai, proving it predated the Sinai covenant (*Patriarchs and Prophets*, Chapter 26: From the Red Sea to Sinai).

**ilk-150 — The Eternal Nature of the Sabbath**
The Sabbath commandment absolutely exists in heaven and will endure for eternity. The original tables of stone containing the Sabbath commandment are securely deposited in the ark in the heavenly sanctuary (*The Great Controversy*, Chapter 25: God's Law Immutable). Furthermore, "the Sabbath never will be done away; but that the redeemed saints, and all the angelic host, will observe it in honor of the great Creator to all eternity" (*Early Writings*, Chapter: Subsequent Visions). When Eden is restored, all flesh will gather to worship God "from one sabbath to another" (*The Great Controversy*, Chapter 42: The Controversy Ended).

**ilk-151 — The Old Testament Death Penalty**
The death penalty for Sabbath violation in the Old Testament—such as the man stoned for gathering sticks—was executed because it was a "willful and deliberate violation" and a "sin of presumption" (*Patriarchs and Prophets*, Chapter 36: In the Wilderness). It demonstrated an open expression of contempt and defiance toward the authority of God (*Patriarchs and Prophets*, Chapter 36: In the Wilderness). While God does not currently enforce temporal death penalties for violating His law, the severity of the OT penalty translates to the final judgment, where "death is the portion of those who violate His sacred precepts" (*Spiritual Gifts, vol. 1*, Chapter: The Sabbath).

**ilk-178 — The Sabbath's Pre-Sinai Basis**
The principles of God's moral law existed before the creation of man and governed the angels (*Spiritual Gifts, vol. 3*, Chapter: The Sabbath). However, the Sabbath itself was specifically made for man after the physical creation of the world (*The Great Controversy*, Chapter 25: God's Law Immutable). It was instituted at the close of the six literal days of Creation Week when God rested, blessed the seventh day, and sanctified it (*Patriarchs and Prophets*, Chapter 2: The Creation).

**ilk-086 — The Shift to Sunday Worship**
Sunday observance originated in the "mystery of lawlessness" as a pagan festival ("the venerable day of the sun") (*The Great Controversy*, Chapter 3: An Era of Spiritual Darkness). In the early centuries, unconsecrated church leaders sought to exalt Sunday to honor Christ's resurrection, attempting to conciliate pagans and simultaneously distance Christians from Judaism (*The Great Controversy*, Chapter 3: An Era of Spiritual Darkness). The decisive historical shift occurred in A.D. 321, when Emperor Constantine issued a secular decree making Sunday a public festival and day of rest throughout the Roman Empire (*The Great Controversy*, Chapter 3: An Era of Spiritual Darkness). Following this, Bishop Eusebius advanced the unscriptural claim that Christ had transferred the Sabbath to Sunday (*The Great Controversy*, Chapter 35: Liberty of Conscience Threatened). Subsequent papal councils systematically pressed down the true Sabbath and elevated Sunday, establishing it entirely upon human tradition and ecclesiastical authority (*The Great Controversy*, Chapter 3: An Era of Spiritual Darkness). Roman Catholic catechisms explicitly acknowledge that the Church substituted Sunday for Saturday, using it as a mark of their ecclesiastical power (*The Great Controversy*, Chapter 25: God's Law Immutable).
