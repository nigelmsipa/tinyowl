**Procrastination (ilk-008)**
Procrastination is spiritually dangerous. The sources warn, "Beware of procrastination," emphasizing that putting off the work of forsaking sins and seeking purity through Jesus is a terrible danger (*Steps to Christ*, "Repentance"). Delaying to yield to the Holy Spirit is actually choosing to live in sin, and what we do not overcome will eventually destroy us (*Steps to Christ*, "Repentance"). "Indecision soon becomes decision in the wrong direction" (*Testimonies for the Church, vol. 4*, "Indulgence of Appetite"). "Delays are virtually defeats" (*Selected Messages Book 2*, "Hasty Decisions"; *Testimony Treasures, vol. 1*, "Folly in Delay"), and the day is fast approaching when probation will close (*Mind, Character and Personality, Vol. 2*, "Vital Interests in Your Keeping").

**Human Nature and Sin (ilk-016, ilk-181)**
Sin is truly universal. The Bible declares that "All have sinned, and come short of the glory of God," and the entire human family is hopelessly ruined and condemned by the law (*Testimonies on Sexual Behavior, Adultery, and Divorce*, "Total Life Control Possible"; *Testimonies for the Church, vol. 3*, "Experience Not Reliable"). Regarding inherited sinful nature, the sources teach that because of Adam's fall, "his posterity was born with inherent propensities of disobedience" (*SDA Bible Commentary, vol. 7A*, "Perfect Sinlessness of Christ's Human Nature"). Christ, however, "took human nature, and bore the infirmities and degeneracy of the race" (*SDA Bible Commentary, vol. 4*, Comments on Isaiah 53; *The Youth's Instructor*, "The Cross and the Crown"). While He was tempted in all points like us and took on our fallen nature, "Christ did not in the least participate in its sin," possessing no evil propensities or taint of corruption (*SDA Bible Commentary, vol. 7A*, "Perfect Sinlessness of Christ's Human Nature"; *Selected Messages Book 1*, "Christ Our Example"; *The Desire of Ages*, "The Temptation"). We are not held responsible for the sins of our fathers unless we follow their evil practices (*SDA Bible Commentary, vol. 4*, Comments on Ezekiel 18).

**Christian Meditation (ilk-027)**
Christian meditation is highly permissible and encouraged. Believers are instructed to "spend a thoughtful hour each day in contemplation of the life of Christ," taking it point by point and grasping each closing scene (*The Desire of Ages*, "Gethsemane"; *The Ministry of Healing*, "Helping the Tempted"). Meditation upon the perfections of the Saviour, His love, and His mercy transforms the soul into His divine likeness (*Steps to Christ*, "The Privilege of Prayer"; *The Desire of Ages*, "To My Father, and Your Father"; *Thoughts from the Mount of Blessing*, "The Beatitudes"; *Christian Education*, "Mental Inebriates").

While the sources do not explicitly use the term "Eastern practices," they warn against "the phantomizing system of the Buddhists" (*The Great Controversy 1888*, "Light Through Darkness"; *From Here to Forever*, "The Fatal Error") and condemn the "theory of mind controlling mind" as a dangerous deception originated by Satan to separate man from God (*SDA Bible Commentary, vol. 3*, Comments on Proverbs 4:23; *Messages to Young People*, "The Domination of Passion"; *Patriarchs and Prophets*, "Temptation and Fall"; *Healthful Living*, "Control of Will"). Biblical meditation is always centered on God's Word and Christ's character, not emptying the mind or yielding it to human control (*Christian Education*, "Mental Inebriates"; *Messages to Young People*, "Necessity of Self-control").

**Tares and Wheat (ilk-029)**
Tares cannot be converted to wheat. In the parable of the wheat and the tares, Jesus "does not point forward to a time when all the tares become wheat" (*Christ's Object Lessons*, "Tares"). They are to grow together until the harvest at the end of the world, at which point the tares will remain tares and be cast into the fire to be utterly destroyed (*Christ's Object Lessons*, "Tares"; *The Great Controversy 1888*, "The Great Controversy").

**Hardships and the Believer (ilk-058)**
God does allow hardships in believers' lives. "Trials and obstacles are the Lord's chosen methods of discipline and His appointed conditions of success" (*The Ministry of Healing*, "Co-Working of the Divine and the Human"; *Testimonies for the Church, vol. 5*, "Testimonies Slighted"). God allows His chosen ones to be placed in the furnace of affliction to refine them, remove the impurities from their character, and fit them for the heavenly temple (*Testimonies for the Church, vol. 5*, "Testimonies Slighted"; *The Great Controversy*, "God's Providence"; *From Trials to Triumph*, "The True Aim of Life"; *Testimonies to Ministers and Gospel Workers*, "Exhortation to Repentance"). We are not to fret, complain, or rebel in trials, for they teach us to lean upon our Almighty Helper (*From Trials to Triumph*, "The True Aim of Life"; *Testimonies to Ministers and Gospel Workers*, "Exhortation to Repentance"; *The Spirit of Prophecy, vol. 4*, "The Time of Trouble"; *Special Testimonies On Education*, "The Lesson Book").

**Law, Grace, and the Romans 7-8 Conflict (ilk-076, ilk-127)**
The law and the gospel go hand in hand in perfect harmony (*SDA Bible Commentary, vol. 2*, Comments on Judges 7:7). The law is a mirror that reveals man's sin, but it has no power to pardon or save the transgressor (*SDA Bible Commentary, vol. 2*, Comments on Judges 7:7; *Steps to Christ*, "Repentance"; *The Great Controversy 1888*, "Light Through Darkness"; *From Here to Forever*, "The Fatal Error"). In Romans 7, Paul states, "when the commandment came, sin revived, and I died," meaning the law exposed his sin and condemned him (*The Great Controversy 1888*, "Light Through Darkness"; *From Here to Forever*, "The Fatal Error"; *The Great Hope*, "The Law and the Gospel"). The resolution is found in Christ: the law drives the sinner to the Saviour, who imputes His righteousness to the believer (*Steps to Christ*, "Repentance"; *The Great Controversy 1888*, "Light Through Darkness"). The gospel does not abolish the law; rather, through faith in Christ, "obedience to every principle of the law is made possible," and "the righteousness of the law" is fulfilled in those who walk after the Spirit (*The Great Hope*, "The Law and the Gospel"; *Faith and Works*, "The Standard of True Sanctification"; *The Publishing Ministry*, "Decisive Action, not Hesitation"; *Testimony Studies on Diet and Foods*, "Reasons for Reform").

**God's Sovereignty and Human Free Will (ilk-080, ilk-081, ilk-082)**
While the sources do not explicitly detail Pharaoh's hardened heart, they explain that the hardening of the heart is the natural result of human choice. "Every resistance of temptation makes resistance more easy... The first resistance to the Spirit's pleading prepares the way for the second resistance. Thus the heart is hardened" (*Character Sketches From the Life of Daniel*, "Nebuchadnezzar's Humiliation").

God's foreknowledge does not mean choices are predetermined. The doctrine of divine decrees unalterably fixing the character of men is condemned as an error; "Men fail of salvation through their own willful refusal of the gift of life" (*The Sanctified Life*, "The Testing Time"; *Confrontation*, "The Test of Probation"). God knew the conditions of the future, but "He did not make His purposes to fit circumstances" or arbitrarily predestine rebellion (*Confrontation*, "The Test of Probation"). God does not force the conscience (*The Spirit of Prophecy, vol. 1*, "The Atonement").

However, God does eventually withdraw from people if they persistently reject Him. If people turn from the truth, "He leaves them to themselves, to be filled with the fruit of their own doings" (*Testimony Treasures, vol. 3*, "Time Rapidly Passing"). As men reject His warnings, God "withdraws His Spirit and leaves them to the deceptions which they love" (*Life Sketches of Ellen G. White*, "God's Purpose in Affliction").

**Following Jesus and Christian Identity (ilk-088, ilk-195)**
Truly following Jesus requires total surrender, self-denial, and obedience. "To live thus by the word of God means the surrender to Him of the whole life" (*A Sketch of the Christian Experience and Views of Ellen G. White*, "The Sealing"; *Historical Sketches of Foreign Missions*, "Importance of Watchfulness and Prayer"). "It will require a sacrifice to give yourself to God... Our will is to be yielded to Him" (*Historical Sketches of Foreign Missions*, "Importance of Watchfulness and Prayer"). It requires keeping the commandments, for "the requirement under the covenant of grace is just as broad as the requirement made in Eden—harmony with God's law..." (*Testimonies for the Church, vol. 7*, "Author"; *Last-Day Events*, "Control the Moral Powers").

Regarding identity, believers are sinners who are pardoned and treated as saints. "In ourselves we are sinners; but in Christ we are righteous. Having made us righteous through the imputed righteousness of Christ, God pronounces us just, and treats us as just. He looks upon us as His dear children" (*Christian Leadership*, "Trusting God"). By faith, the believer passes from being a rebel to a loyal subject, standing "faultless before God" (*Christian Leadership*, "Trusting God"; *Medical Ministry*, "Righteousness").

**Assurance of Forgiveness and the Unforgivable Sin (ilk-097, ilk-187)**
If you feel unforgiven, you must look away from your feelings and trust God's promises. "Feeling has nothing to do with faith" (*Medical Ministry*, "Righteousness"). The absence of happy feelings is no evidence of a lack of sanctification (*The Colporteur Evangelist*, "Entire Surrender to God"). Scripture promises: "If we confess our sins, He is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness. 1 John 1:9. Glorious truth!" (*The Colporteur Evangelist*, "Entire Surrender to God"; *Acts of the Apostles*, "A Seeker for Truth").

The unforgivable sin (the sin against the Holy Ghost) is "willfully attributing to Satan the work of the Holy Spirit" (*Counsels to Parents, Teachers, and Students*, "Failure to Study God's Word"). The most common manifestation of this sin is "persistently slighting Heaven's invitation to repent" (*Counsels to Parents, Teachers, and Students*, "Failure to Study God's Word"; *Life Sketches of James White and Ellen G. White 1888*, "The Third Angel's Message").

**Failing Vows and Questioning God (ilk-098, ilk-108)**
If you are not living up to your baptismal vow, your hope is in repentance. "Those who have put on Christ by baptism... should not set up idols in their hearts," but if they have backslidden, they must return to Him with true repentance (*Life Sketches of James White and Ellen G. White 1888*, "The Third Angel's Message"; *From Eternity Past*, "The Subtlety of Satan's Appeal").

It is not for finite minds to question or accuse God. "We are not to doubt His goodness because we cannot understand His providence" (*Help in Daily Living*, "Forbearance Under Wrong"; *Daughters of God*, "All Who Seek Jesus Find Him"). To admit we cannot comprehend the great truths of the Bible is simply to admit that the finite mind cannot grasp the infinite (*Daughters of God*, "All Who Seek Jesus Find Him").

**Christ's Sufficiency and Perfection (ilk-110, ilk-143, ilk-144)**
While the sources do not explicitly use the term "Last Generation Theology," they emphatically uphold Christ's absolute sufficiency. "We must not think that our own grace and merits will save us; the grace of Christ is our only hope of salvation" (*Christian Service*, "Commandment Keeping a Cloak for Sin"). "Christ is able to save to the uttermost because He ever liveth to make intercession for us" (*Christian Service*, "Commandment Keeping a Cloak for Sin"; *Peter's Counsel to Parents*, "Patience").

Character perfection is required for salvation, but it is only possible through Christ. "The Lord requires at this time just what He required of Adam in Eden—perfect obedience to the law of God. We must have righteousness without a flaw" (*Peter's Counsel to Parents*, "Patience"; *Letters to Young Lovers*, "Words to a Young Man"). However, "when, through faith in Jesus Christ, man does according to the very best of his ability... the perfection of Christ is imputed to cover the transgression" (*Letters to Young Lovers*, "Words to a Young Man").

It is possible to live without sinning through divine grace, but true Christians will never boast of it. "Abundant grace has been provided that the believing soul may be kept free from sin" (*Christian Leadership*, "Trusting God"; *Spiritual Gifts, vol. 2*, "The Temptation of Christ"). Yet, "none of the apostles or prophets ever claimed to be without sin," and anyone who claims to be sinless is deceived (*Spiritual Gifts, vol. 2*, "The Temptation of Christ"; *Christian Experience and Teachings of Ellen G. White*, "Preparation for the End"; *Spiritual Gifts, vol. 3*, "The Time of Trouble"). True sanctification is a daily, lifelong work, not an instantaneous state (*The Colporteur Evangelist*, "Entire Surrender to God").

**Love, Besetting Sins, and Willpower (ilk-111, ilk-112, ilk-167, ilk-215)**
Love is an active principle, not merely an emotion. "The working out of the principle of love is true sanctification" (*Counsels to Writers and Editors*, "Inconsequential Matters"). It leads to obedience and unselfish service (*Spiritual Gifts, vol. 4b*, "The Word of God"; *Darkness Before Dawn*, "First Great Deception"; *Testimonies on Sabbath-School Work*, "Fragments"). Loving God merely to go to heaven is a form of selfishness; those who seek happiness by gratifying themselves "are on the wrong track" (*The Adventist Home*, "The Insidious Leaven of Wickedness"; *Supplement to the Christian Experience and Views of Ellen G. White*, "Christian Fortitude").

It is unchristian to stop struggling with a besetting sin. "Every besetting sin must be put away. Warfare must be waged against it until it is overcome" (*SDA Bible Commentary, vol. 7A*, "Perfect Sinlessness of Christ's Human Nature").

Willpower and moral strength come from God's Word and Spirit. "A firm, decided will-power is not brought into the life and character, because the sacred instruction of God is not made the study, and the subject of meditation" (*The Spirit of Prophecy, vol. 2*, "The Sower"). By believing in Christ, human weakness unites with divine strength (*Pastoral Ministry*, "Prayer").

**Legal Justification vs. Experiential Transformation (ilk-122)**
Legal justification is forensic: "By Him who speaketh truth he is declared righteous. The Lord imputes unto the believer the righteousness of Christ... He transfers his sins to Jesus" (*A Call to Medical Evangelism and Health Education*, "Humility and Love"; *The Health Food Ministry*, "Hold Fast to Your Confidence"). It is God's act of pardoning the sinner and treating him as if he had not sinned (*The Health Food Ministry*, "Hold Fast to Your Confidence"). Experiential transformation (sanctification) is the ongoing work of the Holy Spirit renewing the heart, subduing the natural tendencies, and producing the fruit of obedience over a lifetime (*The Colporteur Evangelist*, "Entire Surrender to God"; *Counsels to Writers and Editors*, "Inconsequential Matters").

**The Book of Life and the Fate of Judas and Atheists (ilk-134, ilk-194, ilk-220)**
Your name can be removed from the Book of Life. Those who do not overcome, or who endeavor to conceal their sins, will be overcome by Satan and face destruction (*Steps to Christ*, "Repentance"; *Testimonies for the Church, vol. 4*, "Indulgence of Appetite"). "All who would have their names retained in the book of life, should now... afflict their souls before God by sorrow for sin" (*Selected Messages Book 2*, "Hasty Decisions").

Judas was not saved. After betraying Christ, he felt remorse, but "there was no genuine repentance for sin, no conversion of purpose, no abhorrence of evil" (*Testimony Treasures, vol. 1*, "Folly in Delay").

While the term "atheist" is not explicitly used, the sources confirm that those who do not believe in Christ will not be saved. "Those who do not believe in Christ are not reconciled to God" (*Mind, Character and Personality, Vol. 2*, "Vital Interests in Your Keeping"). "He that believeth not the Son shall not see life; but the wrath of God abideth on him" (*Testimonies on Sexual Behavior, Adultery, and Divorce*, "Total Life Control Possible").

**Prayer Posture (ilk-145)**
There is a "propriety of varying postures in prayer" and one "need not always kneel" (*Testimonies for the Church, vol. 3*, "Experience Not Reliable"). God looks with pleasure upon those who bow morning and evening, but "If we pray even when our hands are employed, the Saviour's ear is open to hear our petitions" (*Testimonies for the Church, vol. 3*, "Experience Not Reliable"; *Fundamentals of Christian Education*, "The Lord Jesus is Dishonored").
