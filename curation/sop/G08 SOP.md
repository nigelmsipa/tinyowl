**The Special Resurrection (ilk-004)**
At the moment of Christ's death on the cross, a mighty earthquake rent open the graves of many righteous dead who had been co-laborers with God (*The Desire of Ages*, "It Is Finished"; *Early Writings*, "The Resurrection of Christ"). When Jesus came forth from the tomb, these captives came forth with Him as the "first fruits" of His power over death (*The Desire of Ages*, "It Is Finished"; *The Spirit of Prophecy, vol. 3*, "The Resurrection of Christ"). Unlike those raised during Christ's ministry (such as Lazarus) who were still subject to death, this multitude was raised to everlasting life, clothed with immortality (*The Desire of Ages*, "It Is Finished"; *A Call to Stand Apart*, "A Risen Saviour"). They went into Jerusalem, appeared to many as witnesses to the resurrection, and finally ascended to heaven with Christ as trophies of His victory (*A Call to Stand Apart*, "A Risen Saviour"; *Early Writings*, "The Ascension of Christ"; *The Story of Jesus*, "Jesus Ascends to Heaven").

**Messianic Prophecies and Context (ilk-017)**
Christians are not taking Messianic prophecies out of context. Following Christ's resurrection, He Himself walked His disciples through Moses and all the prophets to explain how the Old Testament specifically outlined His life, suffering, death, and resurrection (*The Story of Jesus*, "Jesus Ascends to Heaven"; *Selected Messages Book 1*, "Preparation for the Final Crisis"). The apostles, such as Peter, Paul, and Stephen, consistently used Old Testament prophecies to prove that Jesus was the Messiah (*Sketches from the Life of Paul*, "Paul in Prison"; *The Spirit of Prophecy, vol. 3*, "Paul at Ephesus"). They demonstrated that predictions concerning His descent from David (*The Desire of Ages*, "We Have Found the Messias"; *The Spirit of Prophecy, vol. 2*, "The First Disciples"), His birth in Bethlehem (*The Desire of Ages*, "We Have Found the Messias"), and His substitutionary suffering—such as being "brought as a lamb to the slaughter" and "wounded for our transgressions" (Isaiah 53)—found exact and literal fulfillment in Jesus of Nazareth (*From Splendor to Shadow*, "The Unseen Watcher"; *SDA Bible Commentary, vol. 4*, Comments on Isaiah 53; *Help in Daily Living*, "Pressing Toward the Mark").

**Jesus, Appetite, and Addiction (ilk-048)**
While the sources do not use the modern term "addiction," they describe Jesus confronting the overwhelming, enslaving power of "debased appetite" on behalf of the human race (*Testimonies for the Church, vol. 3*, "Indulgence of Appetite"). Because indulgence of appetite had been strengthening since Adam's fall, causing deep physical and moral degeneracy, Jesus endured a fast of nearly six weeks in the wilderness (*Testimonies for the Church, vol. 3*, "Indulgence of Appetite"). He suffered the keenest pangs of hunger and "felt the overwhelming tide of woe" caused by humanity's lack of self-control (*Testimonies for the Church, vol. 3*, "Indulgence of Appetite"). He overcame on this point to break the chains of indulgence and to prove that through His divine power, humanity can master unnatural and perverted cravings (*Temperance*, "Importance of Christ's Victory Over Appetite"; *Medical Ministry*, "Teaching Health Principles"; *Christian Temperance and Bible Hygiene*, "Christian Temperance").

**Advantage in Resisting Temptation (ilk-049)**
Jesus did not have an unfair advantage over us because He was divine. He "exercised in His own behalf no power which man cannot exercise" (*The Desire of Ages*, "The Temptation"). He met temptation entirely as a man, relying on faith and the word of God ("It is written") just as we must (*The Desire of Ages*, "The Temptation"; *Selected Messages Book 3*, "The Incarnation"). The sources affirm that if Christ had possessed a special power not available to man, Satan would have used it to prove his claim that humanity cannot keep God's law (*Selected Messages Book 1*, "Christ Our Example"). Instead, Jesus proved that through faith, fallen man can lay hold of divine power and overcome every temptation (*Selected Messages Book 1*, "Christ Our Example"; *The Desire of Ages*, "The Temptation").

**The Passover (ilk-050)**
Jesus strictly and literally kept the Passover throughout His life, beginning in His youth (*The Desire of Ages*, "The Passover Visit"; *The Spirit of Prophecy, vol. 2*, "The Passover Visit"). However, His final Passover marked the transition between two economies. The Passover was a shadow pointing to the deliverance from sin through the blood of "the Lamb of God" (*Pastoral Ministry*, "Communion"; *Evangelism*, "The Ordinances"). By celebrating His last Passover and instituting the Lord's Supper in its place, He signified that the typical sacrifices were ending because He, the antitype, was about to be sacrificed (*Pastoral Ministry*, "Communion"; *Evangelism*, "The Ordinances"). When the Jews crucified Him, the entire system of sacrifices lost its meaning (*The Desire of Ages*, "It Is Finished").

**Permanently Human (ilk-117)**
Yes, Jesus is permanently human. In taking our nature, the Saviour bound Himself to humanity by a tie that is "never to be broken" (*Steps to Christ*, "The Sinner's Need of Christ"). He ascended to heaven bearing a sanctified, holy humanity and "forever to retain His human nature" (*Steps to Christ*, "The Sinner's Need of Christ"; *SDA Bible Commentary, vol. 7A*, "Christ Retains Human Nature Forever"). God adopted human nature in the person of His Son, carrying it into the highest heaven, where He shares the throne of the universe as the "Son of man" (*The Desire of Ages*, "God With Us").

**The Only Way to Salvation (ilk-118)**
Jesus is exclusively the only way to salvation. The sources assert, "There is no other way for man's salvation. 'Without me,' says Christ, 'ye can do nothing'" (*Testimonies for the Church, vol. 5*, "Business and Religion"). Because He alone possessed both divinity and humanity, He was the only one who could serve as a mediator and pay the infinite price to reconcile fallen man to God (*SDA Bible Commentary, vol. 7A*, "Heavenly Advocate Retains Human Nature Forever"; *The Spirit of Prophecy, vol. 1*, "The Atonement").

**Christ's Human Nature and Sin (ilk-119)**
Jesus took upon Himself our fallen human nature, bearing the infirmities, weaknesses, and liabilities of a race that had degenerated over four thousand years (*SDA Bible Commentary, vol. 4*, Comments on Isaiah 53; *From Heaven With Love*, "The Wilderness Temptation"; *Confrontation*, "Confrontation in the Desert"). However, He was "born without a taint of sin" (*SDA Bible Commentary, vol. 7A*, "Perfect Sinlessness of Christ's Human Nature"). The texts strongly caution: "Do not set Him before the people as a man with the propensities of sin... not for one moment was there in Him an evil propensity" (*SDA Bible Commentary, vol. 5*, Comments on John 14). He took man's nature, but not man's sinfulness (*Selected Messages Book 1*, "Christ Our Example").

**Relationship with the Jewish Nation (ilk-120)**
Because the Jewish nation as a corporate body rejected Christ and demanded His crucifixion, they sealed their rejection of the gospel, and "Israel was divorced from God" (*The Desire of Ages*, "In Pilate's Judgment Hall"; *The Spirit of Prophecy, vol. 3*, "Weeping Over Jerusalem"). However, God did not permanently reject the Jewish people as individuals. The sources indicate that in the closing proclamation of the gospel, when it is presented in its fullness, many Jews will recognize Christ in the Old Testament, their slumbering faculties will be aroused, and they will accept Him as their Redeemer (*The Acts of the Apostles*, "A Seeker for Truth"; *Gospel Workers*, "Our Duty to the Jews"; *Evangelism*, "The Message Triumphant").

**Raising the Dead in John 5:21 (ilk-121)**
In response to the Sadducees, Jesus declared that "as the Father raiseth up the dead, and quickeneth them; even so the Son quickeneth whom He will" (*The Desire of Ages*, "Controversy"; *The Spirit of Prophecy, vol. 2*, "The Raising of Lazarus"). This affirmed that the Son possesses the very same life-giving resurrection power as the Father.

**Mary's Genealogical Lineage (ilk-131)**
The provided sources trace the descent of Jesus from Abraham and David, but they do not contain any information explaining why Mary's genealogical lineage is missing or incomplete in Scripture.

**Substitutionary Act (ilk-140)**
Christ dying for sins was completely a substitutionary act. He became man's "substitute and surety" (*Faith and Works*, "Christ Our Righteousness"; *From Splendor to Shadow*, "The Unseen Watcher"). "He was condemned for our sins, in which He had no share, that we might be justified by His righteousness, in which we had no share. He suffered the death which was ours, that we might receive the life which was His" (*The Desire of Ages*, "God With Us"; *The Ministry of Healing*, "The Touch of Faith").

**Genuinely Tempted (ilk-190)**
Christ was genuinely tempted. The sources explicitly state: "Unless there is a possibility of yielding, temptation is no temptation" (*Selected Messages Book 3*, "Was Christ Capable of Yielding to Temptation?"; *SDA Bible Commentary, vol. 5*, Comments on Hebrews 4). As a free agent, "He could be tempted, and that strongly, and could yield to the temptations" (*SDA Bible Commentary, vol. 5*, Comments on Hebrews 4). Because of His perfect holiness, His refined sensibilities made contact with evil unspeakably painful, making His suffering under temptation immensely severe (*Testimonies for the Church, vol. 5*, "Business and Religion").

**Sabbath and Health (ilk-191)**
Jesus strictly kept the seventh-day Sabbath, though He actively swept away the "senseless restrictions" the Pharisees had added to it, demonstrating by His healing miracles that it is lawful to do good on God's holy day (*The Desire of Ages*, "The Sabbath"; *The Spirit of Prophecy, vol. 2*, "The Sabbath"; *The Story of Jesus*, "The Sabbath"; *From Heaven With Love*, "Jesus and the Woman With Five Husbands"). He also lived a life of strict temperance and health, denying perverted appetite and observing abstemious habits, establishing a principle that followers should not indulge in gluttony, stimulants, or narcotics (*Testimonies for the Church, vol. 3*, "Indulgence of Appetite"; *Christian Temperance and Bible Hygiene*, "Christian Temperance"; *Temperance*, "Importance of Christ's Victory Over Appetite").

**Was Jesus Created? (ilk-192)**
Jesus was not created. He existed "from all eternity" (*Evangelism*, "The Message Triumphant"). Regarding His nature, the texts declare, "In Christ is life, original, unborrowed, underived" (*SDA Bible Commentary, vol. 7A*, "The Divine Son of God"; *The Desire of Ages*, "Lazarus, Come Forth"). If He made all things, "He existed before all things," acting as God essentially and in the highest sense (*Evangelism*, "The Message Triumphant").

**Jesus and Tolerance (ilk-193)**
Jesus was infinitely compassionate to the outcast and marginalized, but He was not tolerant of sin. He freely associated with the poor, the afflicted, and the despised (*The Desire of Ages*, "Levi-Matthew"; *The Ministry of Healing*, "The Touch of Faith"). However, He "severely denounced hypocrisy of every sort" (*Testimonies for the Church, vol. 4*, "Go Forward"). He was despised and hated by the rulers specifically "because he would not tolerate their sins, and praise, and glorify, and flatter them" (*The Youth's Instructor*, "The Cross and the Crown").

**Where Did Jesus Go Immediately After His Ascension? (ilk-209)**
Immediately following His resurrection on Sunday morning, Jesus ascended to the heavenly courts to present Himself before God. He refused to receive the homage of His people until He heard from the Father directly that His sacrifice had been accepted and that His atonement was ample (*SDA Bible Commentary, vol. 5*, Comments on John 20; *The Desire of Ages*, "To My Father, and Your Father"; *The Spirit of Prophecy, vol. 3*, "The Resurrection of Christ"). After receiving this assurance, He returned to earth that very same day to show Himself to His disciples (*SDA Bible Commentary, vol. 5*, Comments on John 20).

**Where Was Jesus Between His Death and Resurrection? (ilk-211)**
During the three days, Jesus rested in Joseph's tomb (*The Desire of Ages*, "In Joseph's Tomb"; *The Youth's Instructor*, "The Cross and the Crown"). The sources explicitly reject the idea that His soul went to heaven during this time, stating: "The spirit of Jesus slept in the tomb with His body, and did not wing its way to heaven, there to maintain a separate existence... All that comprised the life and intelligence of Jesus remained with His body in the sepulcher" (*SDA Bible Commentary, vol. 5*, Comments on John 10; *The Spirit of Prophecy, vol. 3*, "The Resurrection of Christ").
