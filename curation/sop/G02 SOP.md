**Recreation, Games, and Physical Exercise (ilk-013, ilk-022, ilk-188, ilk-230)**
While the sources do not explicitly mention "biking," they provide clear principles regarding physical activity and recreation on the Sabbath. Wholesome Sabbath rest is distinguished from Sabbath-breaking by its focus and energy. The Sabbath is not intended for useless inactivity; however, it is not to be a day for secular "pleasure seeking," "amusements," or vigorous athletic sports, which distract the mind from God (*The Story of Jesus*, "The Sabbath"; *The Adventist Home*, "How the Christian Chooses His Recreation"). The sources explicitly state that "pleasure seeking, ball playing, swimming, was not a necessity, but a sinful neglect of the sacred day sanctified by Jehovah" (*Testimonies for the Church, vol. 2*, "The Sabbath"). Therefore, engaging in vigorous physical exercise, going to the beach for a general "high time," or engaging in exciting sports are violations of the Sabbath's spirit (*Testimonies for the Church, vol. 2*, "The Sabbath"; *Messages to Young People*, "Recreation and Amusement"). Conversely, wholesome recreation—such as walking in the woods, fields, or by the lakeside to contemplate God's created works and to teach children about the Creator—is highly recommended (*Education*, "The Sabbath"; *Child Guidance*, "The Sabbath").

**Dancing (ilk-039, ilk-040)**
Dancing, in its modern, fashionable form, is entirely condemned by the sources as a "school of depravity" and a device of Satan to cause men to forget God (*Messages to Young People*, "Dancing"; *The Adventist Home*, "How the Christian Chooses His Recreation"). Pleasure lovers often cite David's dancing before the Ark to justify their amusements, but the sources clarify there is no comparison: David's dancing was an act of "reverent joy" and praise to God, whereas modern dancing is associated with midnight reveling, frivolity, and sensual indulgence (*Patriarchs and Prophets*, "The Ark of God"; *Mind, Character and Personality, Vol. 1*, "Dangers Facing Youth"). Because modern dancing destroys a relish for sacred things, no Christian should invoke God's blessing upon the dance hall, and it is strictly forbidden—especially on the Sabbath (*The Adventist Home*, "Lure of Pleasure"; *Messages to Young People*, "Dancing").

**Nature Photography and Hobbies (ilk-061)**
The provided sources do not explicitly address "nature photography" on the Sabbath. However, they do address photography in general, warning that spending money to produce "pictures of our own faces and the faces of our friends" is a "species of idolatry" and a waste of God's means that should be used for soul-saving (*Testimonies for the Church, vol. 2*, "Love of the World"). Regarding hobbies in general, the guiding principle for the Sabbath is whether an activity draws the mind to the Creator or whether it is merely for personal amusement. Time in nature should be spent to "trace therein the wisdom, power, and love of the Creator," not to pursue secular hobbies or "our own pleasure" (*From Eternity Past*, "The Creation"; *Testimonies for the Church, vol. 6*, "The Observance of the Sabbath").

**Pagan Holidays and Easter (ilk-033)**
The sources reveal that adopting pagan calendar observances was a compromise that allowed the "mystery of iniquity" to corrupt the early Christian church (*The Great Controversy*, "An Era of Spiritual Darkness"). To accommodate half-converted pagans, the church adopted the "venerable day of the sun" (Sunday) in place of the biblical seventh-day Sabbath (*The Great Controversy*, "An Era of Spiritual Darkness"). The dispute over Easter arose in the second century when the Bishop of Rome tried to force Asiatic Christians—who observed the Paschal feast on the fourteenth day of the first Jewish month—to celebrate the resurrection on Sunday instead (*The Great Controversy 1888*, "The First Essay of Papal Usurpation"). Eventually, the Council of Nice backed this Roman custom (*The Great Controversy 1888*, "Liberty of Conscience Threatened"). The sources maintain that adopting such man-made traditions or pagan substitutions deliberately sets aside God's immutable law (*The Great Controversy*, "God's Law Immutable").

**Agricultural Work on the Sabbath (ilk-073)**
A farmer must strictly rest on the Sabbath, regardless of weather, crop conditions, or financial pressure. God's command in Exodus 34:21 states: "In earing time and in harvest thou shalt rest" (*Spiritual Gifts, vol. 3*, "The Sabbath"). God knew that during the busiest seasons of the year, farmers would be tempted to labor on sacred time to secure their crops (*Spiritual Gifts, vol. 3*, "The Sabbath"). Intruding upon the Sabbath to save time or property brings God's curse, and the transgressor "will lose tenfold or twentyfold more than they gain" (*Spiritual Gifts, vol. 3*, "The Sabbath"; *Testimonies for the Church, vol. 2*, "The Sabbath").

**Sabbath Preparation and the Holy Spirit (ilk-087)**
For the Holy Spirit to fully bless the Sabbath, believers must intentionally guard its edges and prepare their homes and hearts. Friday is the designated preparation day (*Testimonies for the Church, vol. 6*, "Preparation for the Sabbath"). Practical preparation means that by sundown on Friday, all secular work must be laid aside, secular papers put away, boots blacked, baths taken, and clothing laid out (*Testimonies for the Church, vol. 6*, "Preparation for the Sabbath"). Furthermore, spiritual preparation requires that all differences and bitterness between brethren or family members be confessed and resolved (*Testimonies for the Church, vol. 6*, "Preparation for the Sabbath").

**Kitchen Work and Food Preparation (ilk-124)**
The biblical instruction is to "bake that which ye will bake to day [Friday], and seethe that ye will seethe" (*The Story of Redemption*, "The Sabbath"). The line is drawn clearly: cooking food on the Sabbath is a violation of the fourth commandment (*Testimonies for the Church, vol. 6*, "The Sabbath in the Home"). However, it is not necessary to eat cold food; meals prepared on Friday can be heated up on the Sabbath (*Testimonies for the Church, vol. 6*, "The Sabbath in the Home"). Food on the Sabbath should be simple, palatable, and partaken of in lesser quantities to prevent a "clogged stomach" which causes a "clogged brain" and drowsiness during worship (*Counsels on Diet and Foods*, "The Sabbath Meal"; *Testimonies for the Church, vol. 6*, "The Sabbath in the Home"). Preparing warm food or drinks for the sick is an allowable exception (*Counsels on Diet and Foods*, "The Sabbath Meal").

**Is the Sabbath Burdensome? (ilk-125)**
God's requirements are merciful and reasonable; the Sabbath was made for man's benefit (*The Desire of Ages*, "The Sabbath"). It was the Jewish rabbis, with their "meaningless exactions" and "senseless restrictions" (such as forbidding lighting a candle), who made the Sabbath a "yoke of bondage" and an "intolerable burden" (*The Spirit of Prophecy, vol. 2*, "The Sabbath"; *The Desire of Ages*, "The Sabbath"). God commands believers in Isaiah 58:13-14 to "call the Sabbath a delight, the holy of the Lord, honorable" (*Prophets and Kings*, "Instructed in the Law of God"). It should be the most joyful and interesting day of the week for families (*Child Guidance*, "The Sabbath").

**Commerce, Earning Money, and Lotteries (ilk-130, ilk-142, ilk-146)**
Earning money, transacting business, or pursuing worldly profit is strictly forbidden on the Sabbath. "No labor for worldly pleasure or profit is lawful upon that day" (*The Story of Jesus*, "The Sabbath"). Nehemiah 13 is cited as biblical proof against commerce; Nehemiah vehemently rebuked the men of Judah for treading winepresses and the men of Tyre for bringing in fish and wares to sell on the Sabbath (*From Splendor to Shadow*, "Instructed in the Law of God"). Even discussing business matters or planning future secular work is regarded by God "as though they engaged in the actual transaction of business" (*Patriarchs and Prophets*, "The Law and the Covenants"). While the sources do not name modern terms like "automated billing," the absolute prohibition against financial transactions, worldly business, and secular commerce on God's time makes it clear that engaging in financial tasks violates the commandment (*Testimonies for the Church, vol. 2*, "The Sabbath"). Furthermore, lotteries and gambling are completely condemned as false, artificial, and worldly (*A Call to Stand Apart*, "Danger in Amusements"), meaning participation on the Sabbath would be a grave sin.

**Weddings on the Sabbath (ilk-138)**
The provided sources do not explicitly state whether a wedding ceremony may or may not be held on the Sabbath. They do, however, note that marriage and the Sabbath are "twin institutions" that both originated in Eden for the glory of God and the benefit of humanity (*The Adventist Home*, "Edenic Origins of Marriage and the Sabbath"). Given the strict prohibitions against secular feasting, worldly display, and business transactions on the Sabbath (*Testimonies for the Church, vol. 6*, "Preparation for the Sabbath"), any wedding activities would likely have to conform strictly to the spiritual solemnity of the day, though this specific scenario is not directly ruled upon in the text.

**Allowable Exceptions for Working (ilk-228)**
While secular toil is forbidden, "acts of necessity and mercy are permitted on the Sabbath" (*The Desire of Ages*, "The Sabbath"). Christ's healing of the man at Bethesda and the man with the withered hand demonstrate that it is "lawful to do well on the Sabbath days" (*The Desire of Ages*, "The Sabbath"; *From Heaven With Love*, "Jesus and the Woman With Five Husbands"). Therefore, the sick and suffering must always be cared for, and medical personnel (physicians and nurses) may labor to relieve pain on the Sabbath (*Medical Ministry*, "The Physician as a Sabbath Observer"). However, even physicians must defer ordinary treatments, unnecessary travel, and operations that can wait until after the Sabbath (*Medical Ministry*, "The Physician as a Sabbath Observer").
