## Q&A Pair 1

**QUESTION:**
Good day, I have read Pastor Bohr's notes on Daniel 9 and it says that the theocracy ended after the 70-week prophecy. Then I heard another preacher saying that Patriarchs and Prophets at the very end of its index says that it ended after Zedekiah, quoting Ezekiel 21:25-27. Is there a conflict here or did I misunderstand something?

**ANSWER:**

**No Conflict - The Theocracy Ended in A.D. 34**

According to Daniel chapter 9, there was a time prophecy given and 70 weeks of that prophecy was allotted to the Jewish nation. That 70-week portion began in 457 BC and extended all the way through to the New Testament times, ending in A.D. 34.

**The Chronology:**

**Before the Babylonian Captivity:**
- The Shekinah was in the temple
- According to Ezekiel 8, there were tremendous abominations being committed in the city
- In chapter 9, you have the sealing of those who sigh and cry because of the abominations
- In chapters 10 and 11, the Shekinah departs - it goes above the city and lingers on the Mount of Olives before going to heaven
- The Shekinah abandoned the city

**After the Captivity:**
- The temple was rebuilt
- But the Shekinah never entered the temple
- Some people say "that's when the theocracy ended" - but it didn't
- Haggai has a prophecy where he says that the glory of the second temple would be greater than the first temple

**The Shekinah Returned:**
The Shekinah came to the temple in the form of Jesus Christ. He taught in the temple courts.

**Jesus Did the Same Thing as in Ezekiel 10:**
At the very end of His ministry, He came to the temple and as He got ready to leave, He said "Your house is left unto you desolate." He lingered on the Mount of Olives and gave the prophecy of Matthew chapter 24.

**But Even Then the Theocracy Did Not End:**

**Matthew 23:34** - Even though Jesus says "Your house is left unto you desolate," mercy still lingers for three and a half years.

Jesus says: "Therefore indeed I send you prophets, wise men, and scribes. Some of them you will kill and crucify, and some of them you will scourge in your synagogues and persecute from city to city, that on you may come all the righteous blood shed on the earth, from the blood of righteous Abel to the blood of Zechariah, son of Berechiah, whom you murdered between the temple and the altar. Assuredly, I say to you, all these things will come upon this generation."

Even though the temple has been left desolate, Jesus is saying "I'm still going to give you an additional three and a half years."

After three and a half years, they closed the door of probation for the Jewish theocracy with the stoning of Stephen, and that's when the gospel went to the Gentiles.

**The Book of Acts is Structured Around This:**

**Acts 1:7-8** gives the structure:
- Acts 1-6: Gospel goes to Jerusalem and Judea
- Acts 7: The stoning of Stephen (the end of the 70 weeks)
- Acts 8: The gospel goes to Samaria
- Acts 9: Paul's conversion on the way to Damascus - he becomes the one who takes the gospel to the ends of the earth

**Acts 13:46-47** - Paul and Barnabas are visiting and their message is being rejected by the Jews:

"Then Paul and Barnabas waxed bold and said, 'It was necessary that the word of God should first have been spoken to you. But seeing you put it far from you and judge yourselves unworthy of everlasting life, lo, we turn to the Gentiles. For so hath the Lord commanded us, saying, I have set thee to be a light of the Gentiles, that thou shouldest be for salvation unto the ends of the earth.'"

**Conclusion:**

The theocracy did not officially end until the year A.D. 34. It did not end in the times of King Zedekiah. The Shekinah returned to the temple in the person of Jesus Christ and lingered there as He closed out His ministry, with the promise that the prophets and apostles would come and give them another three and a half years of mercy, of teaching and preaching.

It's amazing how passages that are separated by hundreds of years harmonize like hand and glove.

## Q&A Pair 2

**QUESTION:**
I have a specific question regarding the special group of saints who were resurrected together with Jesus Christ. Of course, in itself this was an extremely extraordinary event which must have attracted a lot of attention and awe from many people who witnessed all this. Therefore, it is to me unexplainable why there is hardly any reports on this event.

Number one: The Bible hardly reports about it. Only the Gospel of Matthew mentions it but nowhere do we find further details.

Number two: I also cannot find any extra-biblical sources of that time like the Jewish historian Josephus who reports about it.

Only in the writings of Ellen White do we find some important details relating to this group - who they were, how large was the group, in what kind of body they resurrected.

If such a thing would happen in our time, it would be world news for weeks or even months. How can it be that this was not widely reported during the days of Christ's resurrection, especially that the Word of God hardly pays attention to it?

**ANSWER:**

**It Probably Was Widely Reported**

How can it be that this was not widely reported during the days after Christ's resurrection? It probably was. It probably was widely reported during the days of Christ's resurrection. That's how it got into the Bible.

The only way that it could have gotten into the Bible was because it was something that was well-known. The Bible writers wrote much later than when the early church events happened. So for them to go back and recount some of these events, it had to have been something that was widely reported.

**John 21:24-25:**

"This is the disciple which testifies of these things and wrote these things, and we know that his testimony is true. And there are also many other things which Jesus did, the which if they should be written every one, I suppose that even the world itself could not contain the books that should be written. Amen."

So there's a lot of things that Jesus did - a lot of miracles, a lot of wonders. Definitely the resurrection of this group of people that went and witnessed was connected with Christ, was connected with His resurrection. Matthew's account says that after His resurrection they came forth, but they aren't necessarily all recorded.

**The Bible DOES Have Further Details**

I beg to differ with the statement that the Bible doesn't have anything more to say about it. The Bible does have more to say about it.

**Matthew 27:51-53:**

"And behold, the veil of the temple was rent in twain from the top to the bottom; and the earth did quake, and the rocks rent; and the graves were opened; and many bodies of the saints which slept arose, and came out of the graves after his resurrection, and went into the holy city, and appeared unto many."

Bible commentaries about these verses are really fuzzy. Everyone has his own speculation about who was in this group. But the passage doesn't tell us who they were, how many there were, when they lived, how they died, and whether they resurrected with their mortal body or with their glorified body.

**Ellen White Provides Helpful Details:**

**Desire of Ages, page 786:**

"As Christ arose, He brought from the grave a multitude of captives."

Now we know how many - a multitude.

**The Word "Captives" Connects to Scripture:**

**Ephesians 4:8** (which is based on Psalm 68):

"Wherefore he saith, When he ascended up on high, he led captivity captive, and gave gifts unto men."

That's talking about Pentecost - when he gave gifts to men. But when he ascended on high, before that, he led captivity captive.

So Ellen White has a basis for saying that they were captives. Of course they were captives in the grave - they were dead.

**More Details from Ellen White:**

"As Christ arose, He brought forth from the grave a multitude of captives. The earthquake at His death had rent open their graves, and when He arose they came forth with Him."

**Who Were They?**

"They were those who had been co-laborers with God and who at the cost of their lives had borne testimony to the truth."

So they're martyrs. That expression "they had borne testimony to the truth" is the identical expression that Jesus uses of John the Baptist in John chapter 5, verses 31-33. It says that he bore witness to the truth.

**Early Writings, page 184:**

"Those favored risen saints came forth glorified."

Now we know that they weren't mortal - they were glorified.

"They were chosen and holy ones of every age, from creation down even to the days of Christ."

That's going all the way back to the beginning - from creation till the times of Christ. And they were martyrs who witnessed to the truth.

**Biblical Support:**

**Matthew 23:34-35:**

"Therefore indeed I send you prophets, wise men, and scribes. Some of them you will kill and crucify, and some of them you will scourge in your synagogues and persecute from city to city, that on you may come all the righteous blood shed on the earth, from the blood of righteous Abel to the blood of Zechariah, son of Berechiah."

So Jesus is saying the martyrs from creation (Abel) all the way till the times of the prophet Zechariah. But Ellen White says that it includes martyrs that lived in the times of Christ - and there's only one: John the Baptist.

**Summary of the Details:**

- They were captives in the grave
- They were saints
- Matthew says they were not a handful - they were a multitude
- They came forth from their graves when Jesus resurrected
- They received their glorified bodies
- They were people that lived from creation to the days of Christ
- They were martyrs who bore testimony to the truth
- They went into the city and testified about the resurrection of Christ

**At Least Three We Can Identify:**

- Abel (the first martyr from creation)
- Zechariah (the last martyr mentioned in the Old Testament)
- John the Baptist (he just took a short siesta because he died during the first year of Christ's ministry - about two and a half years before this)

**Psalm 68 Connection:**

**Psalm 68:18:**

"Thou hast ascended on high, and hast led captivity captive; thou hast received gifts from men, yea, for the rebellious also, that the LORD God might dwell among them."

This psalm was sung while Jesus was ascending to heaven in the procession to heaven (as described in Desire of Ages and connected to Psalm 24: "Open up your gates, the King of Glory will come").

**Verses 24-25:**

"They have seen your procession, O God, the procession of my God, my King, into the sanctuary. The singers went before, the players on instruments followed after; among them were the maidens playing timbrels."

There's this great celebration as the procession is moving into the sanctuary.

**Verses 19-20:**

"Blessed be the Lord, which daily loadeth us with benefits, even the Lord, even the God of our salvation. He that is our God is the God of our salvation; and unto God the Lord belong the issues [escapes] from death."

So in the context of this, all the glory is going to God because He's giving escape. He's leading captivity captive. He's giving all of these captivities to the grave freedom. He's giving them escape from death.

**Conclusion:**

The Psalms are messianic. You get a whole new perspective by seeing Christ in the entirety of the Old Testament. While Matthew's account is brief, additional Biblical support is found in Ephesians 4:8, Psalm 68, and Matthew 23:34-35, with Ellen White providing helpful clarifying details.

## Q&A Pair 3

**QUESTION:**
I have been studying Jeremiah 25, Revelation 20, Ezekiel 38 and 39, as well as Joel 3, looking at the final destruction of the wicked. I have a question primarily relating to Ezekiel 39.

I understand that at the Second Coming the thousand years commences, Satan is bound, the righteous are in heaven, and the dead remain dead. After this, the wicked are resurrected, the New Jerusalem comes down to be set down in the valley created when the Mount of Olives is split in two, and Satan leads the wicked to come against the city, but in the process they're all destroyed and Satan and his sympathizers are no more.

However, this is where I am confused. Following on from Ezekiel 39:9-21 dealing with after the wicked are destroyed, it mentions the saints using the weapons left by the wicked to be used as fuel for seven years, and for seven months the saints shall be burying the wicked. It further goes on to talk about the birds and the flesh.

This is all a bit confusing for me, but I know that God is not the author of confusion. I was of the opinion that the great sacrifice for the birds was before the thousand years (Revelation 19:17-21). Could you therefore please shed some light on Ezekiel 39?

**ANSWER:**

**Dual Applications in Old Testament Prophecy**

A couple things come up when we look at a lot of the texts in Ezekiel and Isaiah that have dual applications - that have a direct application to the people to whom it was written and a secondary application down towards the end of time, an eschatological application.

**Not Every Detail Lines Up:**

One of the things that we do in looking at these texts is the realization that in every aspect of the text they don't line up as far as that secondary application is concerned. You will see aspects that apply to the people at the time, you will see aspects that apply to the time prior to the Second Coming, but in every particular aspect you won't find a parallel.

We see a commonality because we see Gog and Magog in both, but the seven years and the seven months simply does not fit. That is why you have to study the Bible line upon line, here a little, there a little, because you will find places where it just doesn't make sense, it just doesn't fit. So it simply does not apply at that particular point.

**Daniel and Revelation Are Our Primary References:**

In eschatology, for our understanding, there are really two books that are really one. Daniel was actually sealed for the time of the end, and Revelation was given for us to understand rightly the end-time events. These are the two real books that we need to use as a reference point - these are the ones that lead us.

Any other details from other prophets or prophecies that can have dual interpretation in the application are really just side notes that support the overview of Daniel and Revelation, which are the ones that are really for us to understand.

**The Book of Revelation Globalizes the Old Testament:**

What we find in the Old Testament that applied to literal Israel becomes a symbol of what is global at the end of time.

**Example from Joel:**

In the Book of Joel, which is mentioned in the question, it speaks about the valley of Jehoshaphat, which is outside Jerusalem. But in Revelation chapter 14, the same scene where Joel says the harvest in the valley of Jehoshaphat is ripe, in Revelation 14 it says the harvest of the EARTH is ripe. So what was local becomes a symbol of what is global at the end of time.

**Example from Ezekiel:**

In the Old Testament, Gog from the land of Magog comes from the north. But in Revelation chapter 20, verses 8 and 9, it says Gog and Magog come from the FOUR CORNERS OF THE EARTH - from everywhere. So it's globalized in the Book of Revelation.

**The Seven Years and Seven Months Don't Fit:**

We're going to be burying the dead for months or years? That doesn't fit with:
- **Jeremiah 25** - which describes that the earth is going to be desolate and the bodies of the wicked are going to be laying on the surface of the earth
- **Jeremiah 4** - which talks about the earth being without form and void and the heavens not having light

**Principle of Types and Antitypes:**

When the Book of Revelation draws upon the Old Testament, every detail of the type does not parallel what happens in the antitype.

**Example:** Jesus is symbolized by a lamb, but Jesus is not a woolly creature with four legs. Many of the details don't apply. What applies is the general principle - that as the lamb, the sin was placed on the head of the lamb and then the lamb was slain by the sinner. That applies. But not necessarily all of the details.

**Conclusion:**

That's the principle that we have to apply when we take these prophecies in the Old Testament and apply them spiritually and globally in the future. If you try to apply every particular point, you will get yourself spiritually confused. Some points simply don't fit, and if you try to make them fit, you will sort of get off track.

The seven years and seven months in Ezekiel 39 do not apply to the end-time destruction of the wicked. Use Daniel and Revelation as your primary guides for eschatology.

## Q&A Pair 4

**QUESTION:**
I thank our blessed Jesus and I pray for His sustenance of this program. Its benefits are far-reaching. Please, I want to know if Forex Trading is based on the same principle as gambling?

**ANSWER:**

**What Is Forex Trading?**

First, let's explain what Forex Trading is. The Foreign Exchange Market is where currencies are traded. A currency exchange. This international market's most unique aspect is that it lacks a central marketplace - kind of like the stock market. Instead, currency trading is conducted electronically over-the-counter. This means that all transactions occur via computer networks among traders worldwide rather than on one centralized exchange like the stock market.

**Is Forex Trading the Same as Gambling?**

I don't see it exactly as the same as gambling.

**Gambling Is Based on Luck:**

Gambling is based on the principle that a lot of people have to lose for somebody to win. That's the only way you get that big pot - we're all putting in, most of us are never going to see that money again, one person will win or a small group of people. You buy a lottery ticket and you hope you win, and someone else's money that they invested (or shouldn't say invested but spent) goes to you. It's based on hoping that you're going to win.

**Forex Trading Requires Research:**

Forex is not based on that. According to research, Forex Trading takes:
- Research
- Looking at trends in the value of currencies
- Taking into account projections, future projections
- Understanding the financial status of the countries that you're dealing in and their currency
- A certain discipline
- A certain process that needs to be followed in order to make money

There's a science to getting into the market - when to sell, when to hold, those kinds of things.

**A Friend's Experience:**

I had a friend who really spent a lot of time researching and doing his studying in Forex. I had the same questions like "isn't it the same thing as gambling?" I asked him and he said no, it's not. You have to do a lot of studying, a lot of investigation, and really learn how markets work, what currencies are a good investment, and that kind of thing.

He said ultimately there's a level of luck, I suppose, but most of it is really research that you have to do to be able to be successful in this venue. It's not something that you invest little to gain a lot - it's really more little increments and gains as you go through your investments.

**We Don't Necessarily Endorse It:**

I will say that I don't see it the same way. I don't particularly endorse anybody going into these things. I think this is something that if somebody wants to study or get involved in it, they have to really see for themselves if it's something that they want to do. I think there are better ways to earn money - more through applying your strength, your talents.

But if somebody feels like Forex is a way for them to gain investments, I suppose it's up to them. But I don't see them exactly as gambling.

**Conclusion:**

The answer to whether it's based on the same principle as gambling is **no**. Gambling is "you buy the lottery ticket and hope," whereas Forex trading requires research, analysis, and understanding of market trends. It's more in the vein of the stock market than gambling.

However, we're not sponsoring somebody to adventure into those things. If somebody wants to follow because they feel that it's important for them, it's fine, but it's not something that we necessarily endorse.

## Q&A Pair 6

**QUESTION:**
My question is from Galatians 5:23 where it says about the fruit of the spirit: "meekness, temperance: against such there is no law." Since there is no law against the fruit of the Spirit, how do we establish that one has to keep the Sabbath?

**ANSWER:**

**Don't Build Doctrine on One Isolated Text**

The first thing I would say is that we do not build a doctrine based on one isolated text. Number two, there is clear evidence in the Bible that first of all the law is still binding and that we're supposed to keep all the commandments of God.

**Understanding "Against Such There Is No Law"**

Here the issue is with that expression "against such there is no law." If we look at the context in Galatians chapter 5:

**Verse 16:**

"I say then: Walk in the Spirit, and you shall not fulfill the lust of the flesh."

So here we are told that we are supposed to walk in the Spirit, and the way we walk in the Spirit is, or the evidence that we're walking in the Spirit, is the fruits of the Spirit.

**Verse 18:**

"But if you are led by the Spirit, you are not under the law."

This is very similar - this is the same thing as "against such there is no law."

**What Does "Not Under the Law" Mean?**

That expression is used several times in the New Testament by the same author, Paul. One of them which for me is the clearest one is:

**Romans 6:14:**

"For sin shall not have dominion over you, for you are not under law but under grace."

So being "under the law" means being under its condemnation. When we walk in the Spirit and manifest the fruit of the Spirit, the law does not condemn us because the law is a law of love.

**There Is No Dichotomy Between the Holy Spirit and God's Law**

**Romans 8:3-4:**

"For what the law could not do in that it was weak through the flesh, God did by sending His own Son in the likeness of sinful flesh, on account of sin: He condemned sin in the flesh, that the righteous requirement of the law might be fulfilled in us who do not walk according to the flesh but according to the Spirit."

So here Paul is saying that God sent His Son so that the righteous requirement of the law might be fulfilled in us who walk not according to the flesh but according to the Spirit.

**Walking in the Spirit Fulfills the Law:**

When we walk in the Spirit, we fulfill the requirements of the law. The law defines what love is.

**Romans 13:8-10:**

"Owe no one anything except to love one another, for he who loves another has fulfilled the law. For the commandments, 'You shall not commit adultery,' 'You shall not murder,' 'You shall not steal,' 'You shall not bear false witness,' 'You shall not covet,' and if there is any other commandment, are all summed up in this saying, namely, 'You shall love your neighbor as yourself.' Love does no harm to a neighbor; therefore love is the fulfillment of the law."

**The Sabbath Is Included:**

When we walk in the Spirit and manifest the fruit of the Spirit, we fulfill the requirements of the law, including Sabbath observance.

**Conclusion:**

The phrase "against such there is no law" means the law does not condemn those who walk in the Spirit because they are fulfilling what the law requires. There is no conflict between the fruit of the Spirit and keeping the Sabbath. When we walk in the Spirit, we fulfill the righteous requirements of the law through the power of the Holy Spirit.

## Q&A Pair 7

**QUESTION:**
What is the difference between a church elder and a pastor? Are their biblical roles and ordination fundamentally the same?

**ANSWER:**

**Biblically, Elder and Pastor Are the Same Office**

**Acts 20:17:**

Paul calls for "the elders of the church."

**Acts 20:28:**

He tells these same elders to "shepherd" (which is the word "pastor") the flock.

**1 Peter 5:1-2:**

"The elders who are among you I exhort... shepherd the flock of God."

**The Greek Terms:**

- The Greek word for "elder" is "presbuteros"
- The word for "bishop" or "overseer" is "episkopos"
- These terms are used interchangeably in Scripture

**Titus 1:5-7 Demonstrates This:**

"Appoint elders in every city... for a bishop must be blameless."

**Same Qualifications:**

The qualifications for elders/pastors/bishops are the same:
- 1 Timothy 3:1-7
- Titus 1:6-9

**The Practical Distinction Today:**

The main practical distinction today is that:
- A local elder is ordained to serve a specific local congregation
- A pastor typically has credentials that authorize them to serve more broadly across multiple churches or even globally

**But Biblically:**

The role and ordination are fundamentally the same. Both elders and pastors are called to shepherd, teach, oversee, and care for God's people.

**Conclusion:**

Biblically, there is no distinction between the role of elder and pastor - they are the same office with the same responsibilities. The main difference today is scope: local elders serve specific congregations while pastors often have broader authorization to serve multiple churches.

## Q&A Pair 9

Is there any biblical fact on the slavery issue of the black race? The Hebrews were enslaved 400 years in Egypt, and according to history, this is the 400-plus year mark for blacks who have suffered from slavery. Is there anything connecting these two periods?

You will not find any specific exposition of the black experience in America or worldwide in the Bible per se. Men should not wear women's clothing, and women should not wear male‑designed garments to confuse gender. Deuteronomy 22:5 is about preventing gender confusion, not forbidding specific fabrics or styles. Christians should dress modestly and honour God, maintaining gender distinction within cultural norms.

## Q&A Pair 28

**QUESTION:**
If Jesus is our example in all things, why should Christians today avoid eating fish? He ate fish after His resurrection (Luke 24), so is it wrong to eat fish now?

**ANSWER:**

**Dietary counsel changes with circumstances.**

In Bible times fish were a common and relatively clean food. Jesus ate fish (Luke 24:42‑43) and even prepared fish for His disciples (John 21:9‑13). Eating fish was permitted under the Levitical diet (Leviticus 11:9‑10). Ellen White and other health reformers counselled against eating flesh foods—including fish—not because it is inherently sinful but because of health concerns. Over centuries pollution has made many water sources unsafe; fish often accumulate toxins such as mercury and industrial chemicals. God's ideal diet in Eden was plant‑based (Genesis 1:29). After the Flood meat was permitted as a concession, but the goal is to return to the Edenic diet. Ellen White wrote that God's people should seek the best diet for their time; in a polluted world abstaining from meat, including fish, promotes health and clarity of mind. Eating fish is therefore a health recommendation, not a moral test; salvation does not hinge on diet. Christians should make informed choices based on Scripture, health principles and individual circumstances.

## Q&A Pair 29

**QUESTION:**
Why will the saved still eat from the Tree of Life if they become immortal at the resurrection?

**ANSWER:**

**In the resurrection the righteous "put on immortality," but Scripture teaches that immortality is not an innate human quality; only God possesses self‑existence.**

The hosts explain that humanity's eternal life will always be dependent on God. In Eden, Adam and Eve could live forever only by meeting a condition—obedience and partaking of the Tree of Life. After the resurrection we will receive immortal bodies, yet we will go to the New Jerusalem each month to eat the tree's different fruit. Eating is not a burden but a joy and a perpetual reminder that our life is still a gift from God. God alone "has immortality," so the redeemed will forever depend on Him and will gladly pay homage through this monthly act.

## Q&A Pair 30

**QUESTION:**
What is the difference between Easter, Passover and Communion? Is Easter biblically commanded?

**ANSWER:**

**Passover was instituted in Exodus 12 to commemorate Israel's deliverance from Egypt and pointed forward to Christ's sacrifice.**

At the Last Supper, Jesus instituted Communion in place of Passover; He, the true Paschal Lamb, fulfilled the ceremony. The New Testament therefore does not require Christians to observe the Jewish feast days. Easter, by contrast, has no biblical command; it developed later as a tradition to commemorate the resurrection, with its date calculated by the first full moon after the spring equinox. Remembering Jesus' resurrection is good, but there is no scriptural injunction to celebrate Easter, and it should not be used to justify Sunday sacredness. Adventists thus practice Communion at regular intervals while viewing Easter as a cultural holiday without biblical authority.

## Q&A Pair 31

**QUESTION:**
In Genesis 9:26‑27 Noah says that Japheth will dwell in "the tents of Shem." Does this refer to literal tents, and how was it fulfilled?

**ANSWER:**

**The hosts note that in the context of ancient Hebrew idiom, "tents" simply means dwelling places or territories.**

The prophecy foretold that Japheth's descendants would live among the descendants of Shem. Historically the Indo‑European nations (descendants of Japheth) expanded widely, occupying lands previously held by Semitic peoples. European colonization of the Americas, Africa and parts of Asia is cited as a fulfillment of this expansion. Meanwhile, the Shemites conquered spiritually, spreading the gospel. Thus "tents" is a metaphor for habitation and influence, not literal canvas shelters.

## Q&A Pair 32

**QUESTION:**
Why did Jesus instruct His followers to pray that their flight not be in winter or on the Sabbath (Matthew 24:20)?

**ANSWER:**

**Jesus' warning referred to the destruction of Jerusalem (AD 70) and, by extension, end‑time crises.**

When Roman armies approached, believers were to flee immediately without gathering belongings. Winter travel in Palestine was difficult, and fleeing on the Sabbath would hinder rest and worship and might have been more dangerous due to assembled worshippers. The counsel is thus practical, not an admonition to break Sabbath, and it also implies that the Sabbath remained binding decades after the cross. Jesus' words encourage believers to prepare spiritually for coming tribulation, trusting God's guidance whenever flight becomes necessary.

While the program does not provide a full exposition, it underscores that proper study upholds the biblical doctrine of unconscious sleep in death.

## Q&A Pair 34

**QUESTION:**
Has the New Testament abolished the ceremonial law? William Johnson claimed it has not.

**ANSWER:**

**Colossians 2:14 speaks of the "handwriting of requirements that was against us" being nailed to the cross.**

The hosts explain that this refers to the ceremonial ordinances (circumcision, feast days, sacrificial rituals) that pointed forward to Christ. These ordinances functioned like an IOU, continually pointing to the future payment of sin by the Messiah; they could never in themselves remove sin. When Jesus died, He fulfilled these ceremonies and cancelled the debt bond for penitent believers. The moral law (Ten Commandments) is distinct and remains binding. The rending of the temple veil at Christ's death symbolized the end of the sacrificial system. Thus the New Testament does not abrogate God's moral law but confirms that the ceremonial system ended in Christ.

## Q&A Pair 35

**QUESTION:**
At the second resurrection will the wicked come up with their old, diseased bodies? Will bedridden people have to lie on the ground while Satan gathers his army?

We should strive to be part of the first resurrection rather than worry about the details of the second.

## Q&A Pair 36

**QUESTION:**
Did Jesus' divine nature die on the cross? If not, how did His resurrection occur?

**ANSWER:**

**Scripture teaches that Jesus was both fully God and fully human.**

At the crucifixion His human nature died, but the divine nature—being eternal—cannot die. Jesus Himself said, "Destroy this temple, and in three days I will raise it up," referring to His body. In John 10 He declared He had power to lay down His life and power to take it again. The resurrection involved all three members of the Godhead: the Father commanded, the Holy Spirit acted, and Jesus used His divine power in obedience to the Father. Christ truly "tasted death for everyone," yet He conquered death and now offers immortality to us.
